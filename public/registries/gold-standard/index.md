# The Gold Standard PDD: every section and what it must establish

> A Gold Standard for the Global Goals project is documented in the Project Design Document template v2.0 of 15 May 2026: key project information, a project impact summary, and sections A to I. The PDD must show methodology compliance, a safeguarding assessment, a stakeholder consultation, and monitored contributions to at least three Sustainable Development Goals, including SDG 13.

Updated 2026-09-30. Canonical: https://climat.today/registries/gold-standard/

- **Core standard:** Principles & Requirements v2.1, published 31 January 2025, effective 5 December 2024
- **Current PDD template:** Project Design Document template v2.0, 15 May 2026
- **Template structure:** Key project information, project impact summary, and sections A to I
- **Sustainable Development Goals:** At least three, one of which must be SDG 13
- **Certification cycle:** Five years, renewable; 15 years in total for renewable energy and community services, 30 to 50 years for afforestation and reforestation
- **First submission deadline:** Preliminary review within one year of the project start date
- **Review fees (Fee Schedule v3.1):** USD 1,000 preliminary review, USD 2,500 design review, USD 2,000 performance review

## Which documents govern a Gold Standard for the Global Goals project?

Every project applies the Principles & Requirements and the documents associated with them. It then applies the activity requirements for its project type and, if it seeks credits, the methodology and the product requirements. Section 1.3 of the [Principles & Requirements v2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/) sets that order.

| Document | Current version | What it governs |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Principles & Requirements | v2.1, published 31 January 2025, effective 5 December 2024 | Eligibility, the five principles, the project cycle and the project documentation |
| [Safeguarding Principles & Requirements](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/103-par-safeguarding-principles-requirements/) | v2.1, published 29 June 2023 | The assessment against nine safeguarding principles |
| [Stakeholder Consultation and Engagement Requirements](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/102-par-stakeholder-consultation-requirements/) | v2.1, published 14 June 2022 | The two consultation rounds and the grievance mechanism |
| [GHG Emissions Reduction & Sequestration Product Requirements](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/501-pr-ghg-emissions-reductions-sequestration/) | v3.2, published 12 December 2025 | Issuance of Gold Standard Verified Emission Reductions (GSVERs): eligible gases, project scale, crediting period, double counting |
| Activity requirements for the project type | [Community Services v1.2](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/201-ar-community-services-activity-requirements/) (October 2019), [Renewable Energy v1.4](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/202-ar-renewable-energy-activity-requirements/) (16 August 2021), [Land Use & Forests v1.2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/203-ar-luf-activity-requirements/) (April 2020) | Eligibility, crediting period and additionality rules for the project type |
| [Requirements for Paris Agreement Alignment](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/119_paa_pr_100-01-requirements-paris-agreement-alignment/) | v1.1, published 2 February 2026 | The methodology a project may apply for credits of vintage 2026 onward |

A project submitted for preliminary review after 30 June 2026 must apply a Paris Agreement aligned methodology (Requirements for Paris Agreement Alignment, section 4.1.1). Where no activity requirements exist for a project type, the Principles & Requirements apply on their own (section 1.3.2). The PDD template also lists blue carbon and freshwater wetlands, and engineered removal, as activity requirement categories. Those documents are not summarized here.

## Which PDD template is current?

The current template is the [Project Design Document template v2.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/t-prereview-design-document/), dated 15 May 2026. The previous version is v1.5 of 29 June 2023. The template is written for a standalone project activity.

The document history of v2.0 records structural changes, reporting space for new requirements such as Paris Agreement alignment, and specific reporting on safeguarding, stakeholder consultation and gender. Gold Standard's template page says developers may use the older templates for vintages that are not Paris Agreement aligned, and may use the new forms voluntarily in any case.

Section 6.1.1 of the Principles & Requirements requires approved Gold Standard templates for everything except supporting evidence. The template asks for the PDD as a non-editable PDF and sets a file name for each stage, for example `GSID_PDD_DDMMYYYY_VER_X` at design review. For what a PDD is and how other standards name it, see [what is a project design document](https://climat.today/guides/what-is-a-pdd/).

## What does each section of the PDD template have to establish?

Template v2.0 has three unnumbered opening blocks, then sections A to I. The table follows the template's order.

| Section | What it has to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Key project information | Host country, developer, scale, regular or retroactive project cycle, reductions or removals, the activity requirement, the methodologies with their versions, and the product requirements applied. |
| Transition details | Only for a project moving from the CDM, the Article 6.4 mechanism or another standard: its status there, and a declaration that each vintage is issued under one standard only. |
| Project impact summary | Annual emission reductions or removals, the SDGs claimed, and checklists for safeguarding, gender equality, stakeholder consultation and benefit sharing. |
| A. Project description | A.1 overview. A.2 location. A.3 technology or measures, and any existing or former project at the same location. A.4 funding, product ownership and benefit sharing. A.5 eligibility and double counting risks. A.6 alignment with national climate and development policies. A.7 start date, operational lifetime and crediting period. |
| B. Application of methodology(ies) | B.1 methodologies applied. B.2 each [applicability condition](https://climat.today/guides/applicability-conditions/), justified per methodology and tool. B.3 project boundary, sources, sinks and gases. B.4 baseline scenario, and business-as-usual scenario or reference benchmark. B.5 [additionality](https://climat.today/guides/additionality/): prior consideration, regulatory surplus, avoidance of lock-in, financial additionality or a performance-based approach, common practice, ongoing financial need. B.6 non-permanence and risk of reversals. B.7 emission reductions or net removals: baseline and its downward adjustment, project emissions, leakage, parameters fixed ex ante, buffer. B.8 monitoring plan, sampling plan, monitoring management system. |
| C. Safeguarding principles and gender sensitivity assessment | C.1 the risks identified under each safeguarding principle, with monitoring indicators. C.2 the mitigation and monitoring plan. |
| D. Gender equality assessment | D.1 how the project meets the gender sensitive requirements, which are mandatory, and the gender responsive ones if sought. D.2 the monitoring plan. |
| E. Sustainable development contribution | E.1 that SDG 13 and two more SDGs are identified. E.2 the contributions and their alignment with host country objectives. E.3 a monitoring parameter for each, with baseline value, project value and net benefit. |
| F. Stakeholder consultation assessment | A summary of the consultation: F.1 scope, F.2 stakeholders invited, F.3 modalities, F.4 comments received, F.5 how they were considered, F.6 the continuous input and grievance mechanism. |
| G. Contact information of project developer | Organization, address and contact person. |
| H. Appendices | H.1 applicability of methodologies. H.2 background to the ex ante calculation. H.3 monitoring plan details. H.4 changes to a design certified project. H.5 voluntary disclosure of intended co-benefits. |
| I. LUF additional information | For land use and forests projects: land-use history, forest management, social and environmental impacts, and the sites and rights of indigenous people and local communities. |

Several tables in sections C and E are filled in only if the developer has not used Gold Standard's Digital SDG Impact Tool. If the tool was used, its assessment file is uploaded to the Gold Standard Assurance Platform.

## What is specific to Gold Standard?

Three things: a safeguarding assessment, a stakeholder consultation, and monitored contributions to the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Certified design status means the project has met all three and has a compliant monitoring plan ([Principles & Requirements v2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/), section 2.1.1).

### The safeguarding assessment

The [Safeguarding Principles & Requirements v2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/103-par-safeguarding-principles-requirements/) set nine principles, from human rights to environment, ecology and land use. Every assessment question must be answered (section 5.1.1), with yes, potentially, no or not applicable (Annex 1). A *yes* puts the requirement into the monitoring plan. A *potentially* is updated in every monitoring report. Where host country law and the Gold Standard requirement differ, the more stringent applies (section 4.1.2).

### The stakeholder consultation

The [Stakeholder Consultation and Engagement Requirements v2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/102-par-stakeholder-consultation-requirements/) require at least two rounds: a physical meeting and a feedback round of at least 30 days (section 3.6.1). Invitations go out at least 30 days before the meeting (section 3.4.2). A consultation held before the project start date makes the project regular. One held after makes it retroactive (Principles & Requirements, section 4.1.42). A grievance mechanism runs for the project's lifetime (section 3.8.1). The full report, on its own template, is due within one year of the start date (Principles & Requirements, section 4.1.35).

### The SDG contributions and their monitoring

A project must show positive impacts on at least three SDGs, one of which must be SDG 13 (Principles & Requirements, section 4.1.12). Each impact must be a direct, intentional effect of the project, measured against the baseline scenario (sections 4.1.13 and 4.1.14). The Monitoring & Reporting Plan in the PDD states the metric, frequency, collection method and quality control for every parameter (section 4.1.44).

## Which program rules does a developer have to plan around?

Four sets of rules shape the timeline: the crediting period, the deadline for first submission, the certification steps, and the routes for changing a certified design.

### Crediting period and renewal

Certification runs in five-year cycles. Renewal updates the project information and the baseline, unless the activity requirements say otherwise ([Principles & Requirements v2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/), section 5.1.1). The activity requirements set the total.

| Project type | Total crediting period | Clause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Renewable energy | 15 years | [Renewable Energy Activity Requirements v1.4](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/202-ar-renewable-energy-activity-requirements/), section 4.4.2 |
| Community services | 15 years | [Community Services Activity Requirements v1.2](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/201-ar-community-services-activity-requirements/), section 4.1.5 |
| Afforestation and reforestation | 30 to 50 years, chosen by the developer | [Land Use & Forests Activity Requirements v1.2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/203-ar-luf-activity-requirements/), section 3.1.9 |
| Agriculture | Fixed 10 years, unless the methodology states otherwise | Land Use & Forests Activity Requirements v1.2.1, section 3.1.10 |
| No limit in the activity requirements | 10 years, with one renewal | Principles & Requirements v2.1, section 5.1.1(f); [GHG Product Requirements v3.2](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/501-pr-ghg-emissions-reductions-sequestration/), section 10.1.1 |

The VVB's renewal opinion must reach Gold Standard by the last day of the current cycle, and a delay reduces issuance in the next cycle (sections 5.1.45 and 5.1.46). A certified forestry or mangrove project managed as conservation forest can extend its period to a maximum of 50 years through a design change ([Extension of Crediting Period requirements v1.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/117_par_extension-of-crediting-period-luf/), sections 4.1.1 and 4.1.6).

### Deadline for first submission

A project must be submitted for preliminary review within one year of its start date, the earliest date on which the developer committed to expenditure on implementation (Principles & Requirements, sections 4.1.39 and 4.1.49). A retroactive project submitted later is not eligible. Section 4.1.49 does not state the consequence for a regular project. Retroactive land use and forests projects have five years (Land Use & Forests Activity Requirements, section 3.1.14).

### Certification steps

Section numbers refer to the Principles & Requirements v2.1.

1. **Preliminary review.** Gold Standard reviews the key project information and the draft stakeholder consultation report, with a completeness check within 14 days. A draft PDD is required only for a new project type, methodology or product (sections 5.1.4 and 5.1.8). The result is Listed status, which is not certification (section 5.1.12).

2. **Validation and design review.** An approved VVB validates the complete PDD within two years of listing (section 5.1.16). Gold Standard's design review lasts at least four weeks and includes a two-week public consultation (sections 5.1.19 and 5.1.21).

3. **Verification and performance review.** The first verification is due within two years of implementation or design certification, whichever is later (section 5.1.29). The performance review lasts at least three weeks (section 5.1.34).

For the full sequence, see [how to register a carbon project](https://climat.today/guides/how-to-register-a-carbon-project/) and [validation and verification](https://climat.today/guides/validation-and-verification/).

### Design changes and deviations

A permanent change to a certified design needs a design change request: a revised PDD in track-change and clean versions and, unless stated otherwise, a positive VVB opinion ([Design Change Request Requirements and Procedures v2.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/111-par-design-change-requirements/), sections 2.1.4 and 2.1.6). A rejected request blocks issuance until the earlier design is restored or another change is accepted (section 6.1.9). A temporary change, or a deviation sought before submission for certification, follows the [Deviation Request Requirements and Procedures v2.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/110-par-deviation-approval-procedure/) (section 1.1.1). Where no precedent exists, Gold Standard's Technical Advisory Committee decides, in four to eight weeks (section 5.2).

## How Climat handles this

Climat seeds Gold Standard as its own document, with its own section list and its own order, because the drafting order belongs to the registry. For Gold Standard that seed is nine sections: project description, safeguarding principles and requirements assessment, stakeholder consultation report, sustainable development goal contributions, application of methodology, additionality, baseline scenario and emission reductions, monitoring plan, and crediting period. Safeguards, consultation and SDG contributions sit near the front, where Gold Standard puts them, rather than near the back as they do under VCS.

That list is a working decomposition, not the template. The [Project Design Document template v2.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/t-prereview-design-document/) is the authority, and a person maps Climat's sections onto its key project information block, its project impact summary and its sections A to I before anything is submitted. Two things Climat cannot do for a developer are worth naming: the safeguarding assessment and the stakeholder consultation both rest on evidence gathered outside the document — a physical meeting, a feedback round, a grievance mechanism — and Climat can only draft the record of what was done and flag what is missing. It does not run the consultation, and it is neither a registry nor a validation and verification body.

Our [comparison of the two registries](https://climat.today/guides/verra-vs-gold-standard/) sets out where the choice actually bites, and [what a project design document is](https://climat.today/guides/what-is-a-pdd/) covers the material both registries want.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long is the crediting period for a Gold Standard project?

It depends on the project type. Gold Standard certifies in five-year cycles that must be renewed, and the activity requirements cap the total: 15 years for renewable energy and community services projects, a fixed 10 years for agriculture, and 30 to 50 years for afforestation and reforestation. Where no activity requirement sets a limit, the Principles & Requirements v2.1 allow one renewal, which gives 10 years.

### How soon after the project start date must a project be submitted to Gold Standard?

Within one year. Section 4.1.49 of the Principles & Requirements v2.1 requires regular and retroactive projects to submit for preliminary review within one year of the project start date, and a retroactive project submitted later is not eligible. Retroactive land use and forests projects have five years under the Land Use & Forests Activity Requirements v1.2.1. The deadline applies to the first submission, not to design certification.

### Is the stakeholder consultation report part of the Gold Standard PDD?

No. It is a separate document, written on Gold Standard's Stakeholder Consultation Report template. Section F of the PDD template v2.0 holds a summary of the consultation and the final grievance mechanism, and refers to the separate report for the full record. The complete report, covering both consultation rounds, is due within one year of the project start date.

### How many Sustainable Development Goals must a Gold Standard project contribute to?

At least three, and one of them must be SDG 13, climate action. Section 4.1.12 of the Principles & Requirements v2.1 sets the minimum, and section 4.1.13 requires each impact to be an intentional, direct effect of the project. The PDD must identify the monitoring indicators for each impact and define how they are monitored.

### What is the difference between a design change and a deviation under Gold Standard?

A design change is permanent and a deviation is temporary. Permanent changes to the implementation, operation or monitoring of a certified project follow the Design Change Request Requirements and Procedures v2.0 and need a revised PDD. Deviations from requirements or methodologies before submission for certification, and temporary changes that will not last beyond a monitoring period, follow the Deviation Request Requirements and Procedures v2.0.

### What does Gold Standard charge to certify a project?

Under the Fee Schedule v3.1 of 28 July 2025, the preliminary review costs USD 1,000, the design review USD 2,500 and each performance review USD 2,000. Issuance costs USD 0.25 per credit in cash, or USD 0.15 per credit plus 2 percent of the issuance. A registry account costs USD 1,000 a year per organization.
