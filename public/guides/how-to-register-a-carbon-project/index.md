# How to register a carbon project

> Registering a carbon project follows one sequence under Verra VCS and Gold Standard: select a methodology, list the project, consult stakeholders, pass validation by an approved validation/verification body, and pass the registry's review. Credits are issued later, after monitoring and verification. Verra calls the approval registration; Gold Standard calls it design certification.

Updated 2026-09-29. Canonical: https://climat.today/guides/how-to-register-a-carbon-project/

- **Verra VCS public comment period:** 30 days, starting when the project is listed as under validation
- **Gold Standard first submission:** Preliminary review within one year of the project start date
- **Gold Standard validation deadline:** Within two years of listing
- **Verra fees (Fee Schedule v1.2):** USD 1,500 pipeline listing, USD 3,750 registration review, USD 0.23 per tCO2e issuance levy
- **Gold Standard fees (Fee Schedule v3.1):** USD 1,000 preliminary review, USD 2,500 design review, USD 2,000 performance review
- **VCS version in force:** Version 5 released 16 December 2025; VCS Standard v4.7 active until 1 January 2027

## What are the steps to register a carbon project?

The order is the same under Verra and Gold Standard: select a methodology, list the project, consult the public, have a validation/verification body (VVB) validate the design, and pass the registry's review. Credits come later, after monitoring, verification and an issuance request.

The names differ. Verra registers a project. Gold Standard certifies the design, and later certifies performance.

| Stage | Verra VCS | Gold Standard for the Global Goals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Listing | Pipeline listing, as under development or under validation | Preliminary review, then Listed status |
| Public input | 30-day public comment period | Stakeholder consultation in two rounds, plus a two-week public consultation in each Gold Standard review |
| Design audit | Validation | Validation |
| Registry decision | Project review, then registration | Design review, then Certified Design status |
| Results audit | Verification | Verification |
| Credits | Verified Carbon Units (VCUs) | Verified Emission Reductions (VERs) |

## Which version of the rules applies?

Under Verra, two versions overlap until the end of 2026. Verra released VCS Version 5 on 16 December 2025, and its [rules page](https://verra.org/programs/verified-carbon-standard/vcs-program-details/) lists the VCS Standard v4.7 as active until 1 January 2027. The [overview of updates and effective dates](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-version-5-overview-of-vcs-program-updates-and-effective-dates/) gives a date for each change. Updates are effective immediately unless the overview says otherwise. Most design requirements apply to requests submitted on or after 1 January 2027. The new stakeholder engagement and safeguard requirements apply to projects with a start date on or after 1 January 2027.

The steps below name the version behind each rule. Gold Standard's [Principles & Requirements v2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/) have been effective since 5 December 2024.

## How do you register a project under Verra VCS?

Section numbers refer to the [Registration and Issuance Process v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/registration-and-issuance-process-v5-0/) (RIP), the [VCS Standard v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-standard-v5-0/) and the [VCS Standard v4.7](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-standard-v4-7/). Fees come from the [Verra Program Fee Schedule v1.2](https://verra.org/documents/verra-program-fee-schedule-v1-2/) of 15 September 2026.

1. **Feasibility and methodology selection.** The developer selects a methodology listed on Verra's website and checks the project against every [applicability condition](https://climat.today/guides/applicability-conditions/). The version applied must be active on the date the registration request is submitted (VCS Standard v5.0, section 3.1.4). Verra's part is to publish the list of active methodologies.

2. **Registry account.** The developer applies for a Verra Registry account, which can be done at any time (RIP, section 2.1.3). Verra reviews the application. Opening costs USD 750 and annual maintenance USD 750.

3. **Pipeline listing.** The developer submits a listing representation with either a draft project description, to list as under development, or a complete one with proof of VVB contracting, to list as under validation (RIP, sections 3.1.5 to 3.1.7). The fee is USD 1,500. Verra may review the documents, and issues must be addressed within 90 days or the project is inactivated (section 3.1.13). For requests submitted on or after 1 January 2027, the [Corrections and Clarifications of 9 June 2026](https://verra.org/documents/corrections-and-clarifications-cc-to-the-vcs-program-rules-published-9-june-2026/) add a stakeholder engagement plan and an ESG risk assessment, with exceptions for projects that started earlier.

4. **Public comment.** Listing as under validation opens a 30-day public comment period (RIP, section 3.1.17). Verra shares the comments and posts a public summary. The developer must take due account of them before validation is finalized (VCS Standard v5.0, section 3.17.20), and the VVB evaluates the response.

5. **Validation.** A [Verra-approved VVB](https://verra.org/validation-verification/) assesses the [project description](https://climat.today/guides/what-is-a-pdd/), visits the site, raises findings, and issues a validation report and a validation representation.

6. **Registration request and Verra review.** The developer submits the project description, the validation report, the representations, evidence of ownership or of the right to operate, and supporting documents (RIP, section 4.2.3), and pays USD 3,750. Verra sends any findings to the VVB in a project review report. The VVB has 90 days to respond, Verra may run up to three review rounds, and the request is approved or returned (section 4.3).

7. **Monitoring.** The developer implements the monitoring plan and prepares a monitoring report for a distinct monitoring period (VCS Standard v5.0, section 3.4.5).

8. **Verification.** A VVB verifies the report to a reasonable level of assurance, with a site visit at the first verification (VCS Standard v5.0, sections 4.1.9 and 4.1.11). The developer then requests verification approval and pays USD 5,000, of which USD 2,500 is credited against issuance.

9. **Issuance.** After approval, the registry generates serial numbers and invoices the issuance levy of USD 0.23 per tCO2e claimed. VCUs are deposited once the levy is paid (RIP, section 4.4.5). AFOLU and geological storage projects also deposit buffer credits according to their non-permanence risk rating (section 5.1).

### What are the Verra deadlines?

The deadlines changed with Version 5, and so did the date they count from. Version 4.7 counts from the project start date. Version 5.0 counts from the initial crediting period start date, the date on which reductions or removals begin to occur.

| Rule | VCS Standard v4.7, section 3.8 | VCS Standard v5.0, sections 3.8.2 and 3.8.3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pipeline listing | AFOLU projects: within three years | All projects: within one year |
| Non-AFOLU projects (v4.7); energy and industry and geological carbon storage projects (v5.0) | Complete validation within two years | Submit the registration request within two years |
| The same projects, where they apply a new VCS methodology | Complete validation within four years, and within two years of the methodology's approval | Submit the registration request within four years, and within two years of the methodology's approval (energy and industry projects) |
| AFOLU projects | Complete validation within five years | Submit the registration request within five years |
| ARR, RWE and IFM projects (except logged to protected forest), and AFOLU projects of 20,000 tCO2e per year or less | Complete validation within eight years | Submit the registration request within eight years |

The v5.0 registration deadlines apply to requests submitted on or after 1 January 2027, except for projects already listed as under validation on or before 16 December 2025. The v5.0 listing deadline applies to listing requests submitted on or after 1 January 2027. From the same date, a project that does not request verification approval within five years of registration or of its last verification approval is marked late to verify (RIP, sections 4.1.2 and 4.7.1).

## How do you register a project under Gold Standard?

Section numbers refer to the Principles & Requirements v2.1 unless stated. Fees come from the [Gold Standard Fee Schedule v3.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/fees/) of 28 July 2025. Gold Standard also publishes the sequence in its [certification process step-by-step](https://www.goldstandard.org/publications/certification-process-stepbystep).

1. **Feasibility and methodology selection.** The developer confirms that the project type is eligible, selects an approved methodology, and checks the activity requirements and safeguarding principles. The developer opens an Impact Registry account, which costs USD 1,000 a year per organization.

2. **Stakeholder consultation.** The developer runs at least two rounds: a physical meeting, with invitations sent at least 30 days before, and a feedback round lasting at least 30 days ([Stakeholder Consultation and Engagement Requirements v2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/102-par-stakeholder-consultation-requirements/), sections 3.4.2 and 3.6.1). Consultation is due before the project start date.

3. **Preliminary review and listing.** Within one year of the project start date, the developer submits the key project information, a draft stakeholder consultation report, a signed cover letter and the terms and conditions, and pays USD 1,000 (sections 4.1.49 and 5.1.7). Land use and forests projects follow their own timeline. Gold Standard runs a completeness check within 14 days of submission and payment, and lists the project on the Impact Registry if it passes (section 5.1.4). Listed status is not certification (section 5.1.12).

4. **Validation.** The developer contracts a VVB from the [approved list](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/verification-validation-bodies/), hands over the complete PDD and stakeholder consultation report, and notifies Gold Standard that validation has started (section 5.1.13). Validation must be completed within two years of listing (section 5.1.16).

5. **Design review.** The developer pays USD 2,500. Gold Standard reviews the documents and the validation report for a minimum of four weeks, including a two-week public consultation (sections 5.1.19 to 5.1.21). Responses to findings are due within six weeks (section 5.1.22). A positive result gives Certified Design status.

6. **Monitoring.** The developer follows the Monitoring & Reporting Plan and files an annual report for each monitoring year in which verification is not completed (section 5.1.39). Missing annual reports lead to de-certification (section 5.1.40).

7. **Verification.** The VVB verifies the monitoring report. A physical site visit is mandatory at the first verification ([GHG Emissions Reduction & Sequestration Product Requirements v3.2](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/501-pr-ghg-emissions-reductions-sequestration/), section 12.1.4). The first verification must be completed within two years of the implementation date or of design certification, whichever is later (section 5.1.29).

8. **Performance review and issuance.** The developer pays USD 2,000. Gold Standard reviews for a minimum of three weeks, including a two-week public consultation (section 5.1.34). Credits are then issued on payment of the issuance fee: USD 0.25 per credit, or USD 0.15 per credit plus 2 percent of the issuance. Rates are lower for projects in least developed countries.

9. **Renewal.** Certification runs in five-year cycles. To continue, the VVB's renewal opinion must reach Gold Standard no later than the last day of the current cycle (section 5.1.45).

## How long does registration take?

Neither registry publishes a total duration. Most of the elapsed time depends on the developer's evidence and the VVB's schedule, and VVB fees are contracted directly with the VVB. The published periods are these.

- **Verra.** A 30-day public comment period, and 90 days for responses to findings. Section 4.3 of the RIP states no duration for Verra's own review.

- **Gold Standard.** A completeness check within 14 days at preliminary review, a design review of at least four weeks, and a performance review of at least three weeks. Each review ends only when all corrective action requests and clarification requests are closed.

For what happens inside the audit, see [validation and verification](https://climat.today/guides/validation-and-verification/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a project have to be listed before validation starts?

Yes, under both registries. Under section 4.1.5 of the VCS Standard v5.0, the project must be listed on the pipeline as under validation before the opening meeting, and validation cannot be completed until the 30-day public comment period has ended. Under Gold Standard's Principles & Requirements v2.1, validation may start only after the project has achieved Listed status.

### Can validation and the first verification be done together?

Yes. The VCS Standard v5.0 allows validation before or at the same time as the first verification, and the Registration and Issuance Process v5.0 allows registration and verification review to be requested together. Gold Standard allows design certification to be combined with the first performance certification, in which case its review lasts a minimum of six weeks.

### How much does it cost to register a carbon project with Verra?

The Verra Program Fee Schedule v1.2 of 15 September 2026 sets USD 750 to open a registry account and USD 750 a year to maintain it, USD 1,500 for pipeline listing, USD 3,750 for registration review, and USD 5,000 for each verification review, of which USD 2,500 is credited against issuance. The issuance levy is USD 0.23 per tonne of CO2e claimed. Validation/verification body fees are separate.

### What happens if Verra returns a registration request?

Verra notifies the proponent and posts any project review report on the registry. The proponent may address the reasons and submit a new request, and pays the review fee again. A new request submitted within 180 days may use the same versions of the program documents, methodology and templates. If none is submitted within one year, Verra inactivates the project.

### What is a retroactive project under Gold Standard?

A project whose first round of stakeholder consultation takes place after the project start date. It stays eligible only if it is submitted for preliminary review within one year of the start date. The developer must explain why consultation came late and how the comments were taken into account.
