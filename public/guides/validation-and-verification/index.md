# Validation vs verification in carbon projects

> Validation is the independent assessment of a carbon project's design before registration. Verification is the periodic, after-the-fact assessment of the emission reductions or removals the project reports. A validation/verification body approved by the registry performs both, raises findings that the developer must close, and issues a report and opinion that the registry then reviews.

Updated 2026-09-29. Canonical: https://climat.today/guides/validation-and-verification/

- **Validation assesses:** The project design and its forecast, before registration
- **Verification assesses:** Monitored results for a past monitoring period
- **Finding types under Verra and Gold Standard:** Corrective action request (CAR), clarification request (CL), forward action request (FAR)
- **Verra VVB accreditation:** By an IAF member accreditation body with IAF MLA recognition under ISO 17029 and/or ISO 14065, plus Verra approval
- **Gold Standard VVB accreditation:** ISO 14065 under the ANSI-GS Accreditation Program, or UNFCCC CDM or Article 6.4 accreditation, plus Gold Standard approval
- **VCS materiality threshold:** 1 percent for large projects (over 300,000 tCO2e per year), 5 percent otherwise

## What is the difference between validation and verification?

Validation assesses the design of a project before it is registered. Verification assesses the results after they have occurred. An independent validation/verification body (VVB) carries out both.

Verra's [VCS Program Definitions v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-program-definitions-v5-0/) define validation as the independent assessment of whether the project conforms to the VCS Program rules, including the reasonableness of the assumptions, limitations and methods behind a claim about future outcomes. They define verification as the periodic ex-post assessment of the reductions and removals that occurred during the monitoring period, based on historical data.

| Question | Validation | Verification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What is assessed | The project design, in the [project description or PDD](https://climat.today/guides/what-is-a-pdd/) | The results, in the monitoring report |
| Nature of the evidence | Forecasts and assumptions | Historical data |
| When | Before registration, and again at crediting period or certification renewal | For each monitoring period |
| Registry decision that follows | Registration (Verra) or design certification (Gold Standard) | Verification approval and issuance (Verra) or performance certification and issuance (Gold Standard) |
| Site visit | Verra: required. Gold Standard: a physical visit is not mandatory. | Both: required at the first verification. |

The site visit rules come from sections 4.1.10 and 4.1.11 of the [VCS Standard v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-standard-v5-0/), unchanged in substance from v4.7, and from section 12.1.4 of Gold Standard's [GHG Emissions Reduction & Sequestration Product Requirements v3.2](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/501-pr-ghg-emissions-reductions-sequestration/).

## What is a validation/verification body?

A VVB is an independent third-party auditor approved by the registry. The developer appoints and contracts it. The registry approves it, oversees its work and can sanction it.

The [VCS Program Guide v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-program-guide-v5-0/) (sections 2.6.5 and 6) gives Verra the power to review a VVB's work, witness its audits, report its performance to the accreditation body, and suspend it. Gold Standard's [Validation and Verification Body Requirements v3.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/109-par-validation-verification-body-requirements/) make the VVB responsible for the certification decision and Gold Standard responsible for managing the process.

A VVB does not advise on the projects it audits. Verra's [Validation and Verification Manual v3.2](https://verra.org/documents/validation-and-verification-manual-v3-2/) tells VVBs not to offer consulting advice when they issue findings. Gold Standard bars VVBs from consultancy on Gold Standard projects (section 8.7.1.5).

## How are VVBs approved under Verra and Gold Standard?

Both registries require an external accreditation first and their own approval second. Approval is granted by sectoral scope, so a VVB approved for energy projects cannot audit a forestry project on that basis.

| Requirement | Verra VCS | Gold Standard |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Accreditation | By an accreditation body that is a member of the International Accreditation Forum (IAF) and recognized under the IAF Multilateral Recognition Arrangement for ISO 17029 and/or ISO 14065 (Program Guide v5.0, section 5) | ISO 14065 accreditation for greenhouse gas activities under the ANSI-GS Accreditation Program, or UNFCCC accreditation under the CDM or the Article 6.4 mechanism (VVB Requirements v3.0, section 7.4.1) |
| Registry approval | Approval by Verra, active listing on Verra's website, and a signed agreement | Desk-based assessment by the Gold Standard Secretariat, granted for 36 months (section 9.2.2) |
| Auditors | The team must meet the competence requirements of ISO 14065 (VCS Standard v5.0, section 4.1.17) | Named auditors must pass Gold Standard's exam with a score above 75 percent (section 9.4.2.3) |
| Annual fee paid by the VVB | USD 5,000 for one Verra program ([Fee Schedule v1.2](https://verra.org/documents/verra-program-fee-schedule-v1-2/)) | USD 2,500 ([Fee Schedule v3.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/fees/)) |

Verra's [VVB page](https://verra.org/validation-verification/) lists the approved bodies and the recognized accreditation bodies. It notes that some previously approved UNFCCC-accredited bodies may hold an extension to use that accreditation until they receive ISO 14065 accreditation. Verra's [rules page](https://verra.org/programs/verified-carbon-standard/vcs-program-details/) lists the VCS Program Guide v4.4 as active until 1 January 2027 alongside v5.0.

On the audit itself, section 4.1.1 of the VCS Standard v5.0 requires validation and verification to be carried out in conformance to ISO 14064-3 and ISO 14065. Gold Standard sets its own rules in its [Validation and Verification Standard v2.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/113-par-validation-and-verification-standard/), in force since 5 December 2024.

## What happens in a validation?

A validation moves from planning to a desk review, a site visit, findings, closure and a report. The registry's own review follows.

1. **Contract and notice.** The developer contracts an approved VVB. Under Verra the project must be listed as under validation before the opening meeting (VCS Standard v5.0, section 4.1.5). Under Gold Standard the project must be Listed, and the developer notifies Gold Standard that validation has started ([Principles & Requirements v2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/), section 5.1.13).

2. **Planning.** The VVB assesses the risk of material error and prepares a sampling plan and a validation plan. Verra's manual treats these plans as living documents that change as evidence comes in.

3. **Desk review.** The VVB reviews the project document and the supporting evidence and cross-checks them against other sources. Gold Standard states that the evidence is not limited to what the developer provides (Validation and Verification Standard v2.0, paragraph 6.2.1).

4. **Site visit and interviews.** The VVB visits facilities or project areas and interviews staff and stakeholders. Under Verra the audit team selects the stakeholders independently (VCS Standard v5.0, section 4.1.14). Where a Gold Standard VVB does not inspect on site, it must describe the alternative means and justify them (paragraph 6.3.4).

5. **Findings.** The VVB issues its findings in writing.

6. **Closure.** The developer answers each finding by changing the design, correcting the document, or supplying explanations or evidence. The VVB decides whether the answer closes it.

7. **Technical review and report.** Gold Standard requires a technical review of the final draft by a team separate from the auditors before the report is issued (VVB Requirements v3.0, section 8.4). The VVB then issues the validation report and opinion.

8. **Registry review.** Verra may send findings to the VVB in a project review report. The VVB has 90 days to respond and Verra may run up to three rounds ([Registration and Issuance Process v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/registration-and-issuance-process-v5-0/), section 4.3). Gold Standard's design review lasts at least four weeks, and responses to its findings are due within six weeks.

Under Verra, validation cannot be completed until the 30-day public comment period has ended. The sequence around the audit is in [how to register a carbon project](https://climat.today/guides/how-to-register-a-carbon-project/).

## What types of findings are there?

Verra and Gold Standard use the same three: the corrective action request, the clarification request and the forward action request. The definitions below are Gold Standard's, from paragraphs 6.3.14 to 6.3.17 of its Validation and Verification Standard v2.0.

| Finding | When it is raised | What closes it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Corrective action request (CAR) | A mistake affects the project's ability to achieve real, measurable, verifiable and additional reductions; a requirement has not been met; or there is a risk that reductions cannot be monitored or calculated | A change to the project design, a corrected document, or explanations or evidence that satisfy the VVB |
| Clarification request (CL) | Information is insufficient or not clear enough to determine whether a requirement has been met | The same |
| Forward action request (FAR) | An issue of project implementation needs review at the first verification. It cannot concern a requirement for design certification | Review at a later verification. If unresolved, the VVB considers converting it to a CAR |

Verra's manual defines a CL as a case where reporting lacks transparency and more information is needed, and a CAR as a material discrepancy or non-conformance. It requires every CL and CAR to be resolved before a positive opinion. The [VCS Validation Report Template v5.0A](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-validation-report-template-v5-0a/) requires the VVB to list every CAR, CL and FAR. The VCS documents read for this guide use the term FAR without defining it.

Gold Standard's own reviews can also raise non-conformities and observations (Principles & Requirements v2.1, section 5.1.10).

## How can developers prepare so fewer findings are raised?

Prepare for the checks the standards tell the VVB to make. No registry publishes data on how far any one measure reduces findings, so treat this list as a reading of the rules, not a guarantee.

- **Check the deadline and the methodology version before contracting.** Verra's manual asks VVBs to confirm both in a pre-validation assessment.

- **Hand over a complete file.** Gold Standard lets validation start only after the VVB has the full project documentation and supporting documents.

- **Cite a source for every value.** The VVB cross-checks the document against outside sources.

- **Keep the documents consistent.** Gold Standard requires figures to align across the PDD, the stakeholder consultation report, the SDG Impact Tool and the validation report (paragraph 7.16.6).

- **Answer public comments in writing.** The VVB evaluates the response (VCS Standard v5.0, section 4.1.6).

- **Do not expect the VVB to propose the fix.** It is not allowed to consult.

## What do the final validation report and statement contain?

The report records the process, the findings and their resolution, and the VVB's opinion. Both registries prescribe a template.

The VCS template has four parts: introduction, validation process, validation findings and validation opinion. Appendices list every finding and the competence of the team. Under section 4.1.24 of the VCS Standard v5.0, the opinion must state its date, the project name, the GHG statement and the period it covers, and that the statement is the responsibility of the project proponent. It must identify the objectives, scope and criteria, say whether the data are hypothetical, projected or historical, and give the VVB's conclusion. For a forecast, it must explain that actual results may differ. The VVB also signs a validation representation, a deed that accompanies the report.

Gold Standard's [validation report template v1.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/t-paa-vvb-project-activity-validation-report/) dates from 15 May 2026. Under paragraphs 7.16.3 to 7.16.6 of the Validation and Verification Standard v2.0, the report carries a positive or negative decision, a statement on the likelihood of achieving the estimated reductions and SDG impacts, the responses to CARs and CLs, the FARs identified, the documents reviewed, the interviewees, and the audit team.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the same VVB validate and verify a project?

Under section 4.1.27 of the VCS Standard v5.0, the same VVB may do the initial validation and the first verification, the next verification must go to a different VVB, and no VVB may verify more than six consecutive years of a project's reductions or removals. Gold Standard allows the same VVB to do both unless a methodology or product requirement says otherwise. Its Validation and Verification Standard v2.0 then requires a different audit team for the verification, except where design certification and the first verification are combined.

### Is a site visit required for validation?

Under Verra, yes: section 4.1.10 of the VCS Standard v5.0 requires a site visit at validation and at crediting period renewal. Under Gold Standard, a physical site visit is not mandatory at validation but is mandatory at the first verification, and the first physical visit must take place within two years of the project start date.

### What is the difference between a CAR and a CL?

A corrective action request says something is wrong: a requirement has not been met or an error affects the claimed reductions. A clarification request says the information is insufficient or unclear, so the auditor cannot yet tell whether the requirement is met. Both must be closed before a positive validation opinion is issued.

### Can a project be approved with an open forward action request?

Yes. Under Gold Standard's Validation and Verification Standard v2.0, a FAR flags an implementation issue for review at the first verification and is reported in the validation report. It cannot be used for a requirement the project must meet to achieve design certification. If it is still unresolved at verification, it becomes grounds for a corrective action request.

### Does the registry repeat the validation?

No. Verra reviews whether the VVB has appropriately assessed the project and fully described that assessment, and its Program Guide v5.0 states that reviews do not replace validation or verification. Gold Standard reviews every positive report with at least one expert reviewer and may raise further findings, but it cannot overturn a negative opinion from a VVB.
