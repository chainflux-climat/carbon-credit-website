# What is additionality in a carbon project?

> Additionality means a carbon project's emission reductions or removals exceed business as usual and the activity would not have happened without carbon revenue. The applied methodology sets the test: regulatory surplus plus investment analysis or barrier analysis and common practice analysis, or a performance benchmark or positive list. Verra's current tool is VT0008 Additionality Assessment, v1.0.

Updated 2026-09-29. Canonical: https://climat.today/guides/additionality/

- **Applies to:** Verra VCS and Gold Standard for the Global Goals
- **Verra's current tool:** VT0008 Additionality Assessment, v1.0, active since 14 October 2024
- **VT0008 steps:** Identify alternatives, barrier analysis, investment analysis, common practice analysis
- **Common practice in VT0008:** Factor F above 20% and more than 3 similar projects without essential distinctions
- **VCS regulatory surplus:** VCS Standard v4.7 wording for requests before 1 January 2027, v5.0 wording from that date
- **Gold Standard rule:** Principle 5 of the Principles & Requirements, v2.1

## What is additionality, and why do registries require it?

A project is additional when its emission reductions or removals exceed what business as usual would deliver, and the activity would not have happened without the incentive of carbon revenue. Verra defines it this way in section 3.13 of the [VCS Standard, v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-standard-v5-0/), and in section 3.14 of the [VCS Standard, v4.7](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-standard-v4-7/). Gold Standard's version, in paragraph 4.1.47 of its [Principles & Requirements, v2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/), is that a project's impacts must go beyond those that would have occurred without the certified project.

Verra's stated reason is that additionality shows a credit represents a net environmental benefit and a real reduction or removal, which is what allows it to be used to offset an emission.

The methodology sets the test. The developer does not choose it. Both versions of the VCS Standard require additionality to be demonstrated under the applied methodology, and section 3.5 of the [VCS Methodology Requirements, v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-methodology-requirements-v5-0/), makes every methodology use a project method, a performance method or an activity method.

## Which additionality tests are in use, and what does each one ask for?

Seven tests are named in the VCS Methodology Requirements, v5.0, and in Verra's tool VT0008. A project method combines regulatory surplus with investment or barrier analysis and common practice analysis. A standardized method replaces the project-specific analysis with a performance benchmark or a positive list.

| Test | Question | What the rule asks for | Evidence to prepare |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Regulatory surplus | Is the activity required by law? | VCS Standard, v4.7: not mandated by any law or, in UNFCCC non-Annex I countries, by any systematically enforced law. VCS Standard, v5.0: the split is high-income countries against all others. | The laws reviewed and how each applies. For a non-enforcement claim, outside evidence of enforcement practice. |
| Investment analysis: benchmark | Does the project miss a required return without carbon revenue? | VT0008, section 5.4.2: the indicator is project IRR or equity IRR. Project IRR is set against the weighted average cost of capital, equity IRR against the required return on equity. | The financial model as a spreadsheet, inputs dated to the investment decision, and a sensitivity analysis. |
| Investment analysis: comparison | Is another realistic alternative more attractive? | VT0008, section 5.4.1: calculate one indicator, such as IRR, NPV or levelized cost, for every alternative and rank them. | One model per alternative, built on consistent assumptions. |
| Barrier analysis | Does a barrier block the project but not an alternative? | VT0008, section 5.3: financial, information or institutional barriers, unless the methodology names others. Carbon revenue must be decisive in overcoming each one. | Legislation, public studies, statistics and market data. Internal papers can support the case but cannot carry it. |
| Common practice analysis | Has the activity already spread without carbon finance? | VT0008, section 5.5: count similar projects in the area. The project is common practice where factor F exceeds 20% and more than 3 similar projects show no essential distinction. | The list of similar projects with sources, and proof of each distinction claimed. |
| Performance benchmark | Does the project outperform a set level? | VCS Methodology Requirements, v5.0: performance per unit of output, input or sequestration must beat the benchmark in the methodology. | Monitored data for the benchmark metric. |
| Positive list | Is the activity type already deemed additional? | VCS Methodology Requirements, v5.0: the methodology qualifies the activity class by activity penetration of no more than 5%, by financial feasibility or by revenue streams. | Evidence against each applicability condition that defines the list. |

The v5.0 wording on regulatory surplus applies to registration requests, crediting period renewal requests and verification requests that include a baseline reassessment, where submitted on or after 1 January 2027, under Verra's [overview of Version 5 effective dates](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-version-5-overview-of-vcs-program-updates-and-effective-dates/). Earlier requests use the v4.7 wording unless the proponent opts into Version 5.

## How does Verra's additionality tool, VT0008, work?

[VT0008 Additionality Assessment, v1.0](https://verra.org/methodologies/vt0008-additionality-assessment/), is Verra's current project-method tool. It has been active since 14 October 2024 and no later version is listed. The [tool](https://verra.org/documents/vt0008-additionality-assessment-v1-0/) replaces CDM TOOL01 for projects under the VCS Program. It names as its sources TOOL01 v7.0, TOOL24 v3.1, TOOL27 v14.0 and the CDM guidelines on barriers.

The tool runs in four steps:

1. **Identify alternatives.** List realistic alternatives, including the project without registration, and drop those that breach enforced law. Skip this step where the methodology sets its own procedure.

2. **Barrier analysis.** Show that a barrier blocks the project, does not block at least one alternative, and that carbon revenue is decisive.

3. **Investment analysis.** Run an investment comparison or a benchmark analysis, each with a sensitivity analysis.

4. **Common practice analysis.** Show that the activity has not already spread in the sector and area.

A project completes Step 2 or Step 3, unless the methodology says otherwise, and then Step 4. Regulatory surplus is shown separately under the VCS Standard. The default area for the analysis is the whole host country, and a smaller area has to be justified.

Three points matter in practice. The simple cost analysis of the CDM tool is gone. A benchmark analysis that only shows the project misses the benchmark still passes Step 3, but the tool warns that its credits may not qualify for Core Carbon Principles labels unless carbon revenue also lifts the return decisively to the benchmark. The investment model is filed as a spreadsheet with readable formulas and published with the project description.

Methodologies can use part of the tool. [VMR0017](https://climat.today/methodologies/vmr0017/), in [version 1.0](https://verra.org/documents/vmr0017-grid-connected-electricity-generation-from-renewable-sources/), requires Steps 3 and 4 and states that barrier analysis is not applicable. [VM0047](https://climat.today/methodologies/vm0047/), in [version 1.1](https://verra.org/documents/vm0047-afforestation-reforestation-and-revegetation-v1-1/), takes its investment analysis from Step 3. For the census-based approach it sets its own common practice test, under which an adoption rate of 15% or more means the activity is not additional. A project whose methodology still cites CDM TOOL01 may apply VT0008 until that methodology is revised.

## How does Gold Standard handle additionality?

Gold Standard treats it as Principle 5, Financial Additionality and Ongoing Financial Need. Under the Principles & Requirements, v2.1, a project uses a UNFCCC-approved or Gold Standard-approved additionality tool, in the latest version available at first submission. The activity requirements then add shortcuts:

- [Community Services Activity Requirements, v1.2](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/201-ar-community-services-activity-requirements/): projects on the positive list in Annex B, projects in least developed countries, small island developing states or landlocked developing countries, and microscale projects are deemed additional.

- [Renewable Energy Activity Requirements, v1.4](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/202-ar-renewable-energy-activity-requirements/): a microscale project is deemed additional if it meets one of the listed criteria.

- [Land Use & Forests Activity Requirements, v1.2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/203-ar-luf-activity-requirements/): CDM tools, a positive list, or, for agriculture projects under 60,000 tCO2e a year, adoption by less than 5% of farmers.

Gold Standard also tests timing. A project must reach preliminary review within one year of its start date, and land use projects follow the limit in their own activity requirements. At design certification renewal the developer shows ongoing financial need with a narrative and an overview of project finances.

The rules for methodologies are tightening. The [Requirements for Additionality Demonstration, v1.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/447-requirements-for-additionality-demonstration/), in force since 1 July 2025, bind methodologies and not project documents. Each methodology must cover regulatory surplus, lock-in risk and common practice, plus financial viability analysis as the default, or barrier or performance analysis. The [Project Design Document template, v2.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/t-prereview-design-document/), has headings for regulatory surplus, avoidance of lock-in, financial additionality or a performance-based approach, and common practice analysis. Under the [Requirements for Paris Agreement Alignment, v1.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/119_paa_pr_100-01-requirements-paris-agreement-alignment/), credits of vintage 2026 or later must come from a Paris Agreement-aligned methodology.

## What do validators challenge most often?

Neither registry publishes a ranking of additionality findings, so no frequency can be stated. The rules do show where a validation and verification body (VVB) has to test the case. These are the points to get right first.

- **Inputs dated after the decision.** VT0008 requires inputs and the benchmark to be valid at the time of the investment decision. Costs incurred before the decision are excluded.

- **A model investors never saw.** Assumptions must match what was presented to management, investors and lenders.

- **A mismatched benchmark.** Cash flows are post-tax, and the benchmark has to fit the IRR type.

- **A thin sensitivity analysis.** Every variable above 20% of total costs or revenues is varied, by at least 10% either way where no better data exist. If a scenario passes the benchmark, the VVB must assess how likely that scenario is.

- **Barriers proven only from the inside.** Internal documents alone do not establish a barrier, and a barrier that money can solve belongs in the investment analysis as a cost.

- **A convenient comparison set.** Any distinction from similar projects must be essential and verifiable.

- **Unenforced law.** Under the VCS Standard, v5.0, the claim needs authoritative, current evidence and is closed to projects in high-income countries.

Verra's [Validation and Verification Manual, v3.2](https://verra.org/documents/validation-and-verification-manual-v3-2/), tells VVBs to ask whether the analysis method suits the project, whether the indicator rests on objective evidence, and how sensitive the result is. It dates from 2016 and predates VT0008. Gold Standard's [Validation and Verification Standard, v2.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/113-par-validation-and-verification-standard/), requires the VVB to assess the evidence critically, using local, sectoral and financial knowledge. How much variation is reasonable, and which distinctions are essential, are judgments for the VVB and the registry.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which additionality tool does Verra use now?

Verra's tool is VT0008 Additionality Assessment, v1.0, active since 14 October 2024. It replaces CDM TOOL01 for projects under the VCS Program. A project applies it only as far as its methodology requires, because methodologies may use parts of the tool or set their own procedure.

### Do I need both a barrier analysis and an investment analysis under VT0008?

No. VT0008 requires either Step 2, barrier analysis, or Step 3, investment analysis, unless the methodology says otherwise, followed by Step 4, common practice analysis. Some methodologies remove the choice. VMR0017 v1.0 requires investment analysis and states that barrier analysis is not applicable.

### When is a project common practice under VT0008?

A project is common practice where factor F is greater than 20% and the number of similar projects without essential distinctions is greater than 3. F is 1 minus the ratio of similar projects with essential distinctions to all similar projects. A methodology may set a different threshold, and a project found to be common practice is not additional.

### Does a positive list remove the need to prove additionality?

It removes the project-specific analysis, not the evidence. Under a VCS activity method the methodology demonstrates additionality for the whole class of activity, and the project shows that it meets the applicability conditions that define the list. Regulatory surplus still has to be demonstrated.

### Is additionality reassessed when the crediting period is renewed?

Partly. Under the VCS Standard, v4.7, a full reassessment is not required at renewal unless the methodology says so, but regulatory surplus must be shown again. The VCS Standard, v5.0, also requires regulatory surplus at verification with baseline reassessment. Gold Standard requires a demonstration of ongoing financial need at design certification renewal.

### Can internal board minutes prove a barrier?

Not alone. VT0008 allows written documentation from the project proponent, such as board minutes or internal feasibility studies, to support a barrier analysis, but it must not be the only evidence provided. The tool expects verifiable outside evidence such as legislation, public studies, statistics or market data.
