# Applicability conditions: how to tell whether a methodology fits

> Applicability conditions are the criteria in a carbon methodology that decide which project activities may use it, such as geography, technology, scale, dates and land-use history. A project must meet every condition, including those in referenced tools and modules, and justify each one separately in the project document. Validators test each condition against evidence.

- **Defined in:** VCS Methodology Requirements, v5.0, section 3.2
- **Where they sit:** Section 4 of VCS methodologies, plus the conditions of every referenced tool and module
- **Assessed:** At validation, condition by condition, by the validation and verification body

## What are applicability conditions, and where do they sit in a methodology?

Applicability conditions are the criteria in a methodology that decide which project activities may use it. Section 3.2 of the [VCS Methodology Requirements, v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-methodology-requirements-v5-0/), gives geography, technology type and historical land use as examples. The same section says the conditions must not contain procedures or obligations. They are tests of eligibility, written so that eligibility can be determined at validation.

In VCS methodologies such as VM0047 and VMR0017 they sit in Section 4. [VM0047](https://climat.today/methodologies/vm0047/) version 1.1 splits them into general conditions, conditions for each quantification approach, and exclusion conditions. The list does not end there. Conditions set out in any tool or module the methodology uses also apply.

Geography can sit in a second place. Section 2.1.1 of the [VCS Standard, v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-standard-v5-0/), sets default eligibility by location for certain activities, such as grid-connected wind and solar. A geographic condition in the methodology takes precedence over that default.

## Why must each condition be addressed explicitly in the project document?

The registry's template requires it, and the validator has to check the conditions one at a time. Verra's project description template tells the proponent to address each applicability condition separately, in a table that pairs the condition with a justification, repeated for every methodology, module and tool applied. Gold Standard's [Project Design Document template, v2.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/t-prereview-design-document/), has the same table, plus one for host country requirements.

A missing condition is not a small gap. Verra's [Validation and Verification Manual, v3.2](https://verra.org/documents/validation-and-verification-manual-v3-2/), treats failure to conform to any applicability condition as a material discrepancy.

Verra issued two templates on 9 June 2026, both effective 1 January 2027 according to its [rules page](https://verra.org/programs/verified-carbon-standard/vcs-program-details/). [Version 5.0A](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-project-description-template-v5-0a/) is for projects with a start date before 1 January 2027. [Version 5.0B](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-project-description-template-v5-0b/) is for projects starting on or after that date, and for earlier projects that renew or reassess the baseline from 1 January 2030. The applicability table is the same in both. For a submission before 2027, confirm on the rules page which template is in force that day.

## How do validators assess applicability conditions?

Condition by condition, against evidence. The Verra manual requires the validation and verification body (VVB) to assess the project against each condition. It dates from 2016, and the VCS Standard, v5.0, still directs VVBs to follow it.

Gold Standard spells out the method in paragraphs 7.12.4 to 7.12.6 of its [Validation and Verification Standard, v2.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/113-par-validation-and-verification-standard/). The VVB validates the documents cited in the design document, checks that they are correctly quoted and interpreted, cross-checks against other credible sources it knows of, describes the steps taken for each condition, and states an opinion. Gold Standard also screens earlier: under the [Principles & Requirements, v2.1](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/101-par-principles-requirements/), the preliminary review covers the applicability of the methodology.

The VVB is not the last reader. Under the [Registration and Issuance Process, v5.0](https://verra.org/documents/registration-and-issuance-process-v5-0/), Verra reviews every project request and can return or reject it.

## Which conditions are objective, and which need judgment?

A condition is objective when a number, a date or a published category settles it. It needs judgment when it turns on a description that the facts have to be matched to.

| Kind of condition | Example from a published methodology | What settles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Threshold | Planting density does not exceed 50 planting units per hectare (VM0047 v1.1, census-based approach). | The census of planting units and the mapped area of the instance. |
| Threshold | Hydroelectric capacity of 15 MW or less (VMR0017 v1.0). | Rated or authorized capacity, whichever is higher. |
| Date or time window | Where site preparation starts the project, carbon stock estimates at time zero are set no more than two years before the start date (VM0047 v1.1, area-based approach). | Dated plot records. |
| Land-use history | The land was not managed forest at any point in the 10 years before the start date (VM0047 v1.1, area-based exclusion). | Evidence of land cover and management across the whole period. |
| Geography | Wind, geothermal and terrestrial solar PV only in low-, lower middle- and upper middle-income countries (VMR0017 v1.0). | The World Bank income classification. |
| Judgment | No woody biomass serving a similar purpose to the planting units was removed in the last 10 years (VM0047 v1.1, census-based exclusion). | Pre-project photos or an attestation, and a finding on what purpose the earlier vegetation served. |
| Judgment | A battery system and the power plant are part of the same investment decision (VMR0017 v1.0). | Ownership, financing and board records, read together. |

Objective conditions still rest on definitions. Continuous cropping in VM0047 means no fallow longer than one season, shown over 10 or more years before the start date. Where the text leaves room, the reading that counts is the VVB's and then the registry's, and neither is bound by a developer's interpretation.

## What can you do when a project fails one condition?

Under the VCS Program a failed applicability condition cannot be deviated from or exempted. The [VCS Standard, v4.7](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-standard-v4-7/), in section 3.20, and the VCS Standard, v5.0, in section 3.19, allow methodology deviations for monitoring and measurement only. Verra's [Exemption Request Procedure, v1.0](https://verra.org/documents/exemption-request-procedure-v1-0/), covers deadlines and VVB matters and nothing else. The real options are these:

1. **Check the input and the definition.** Conditions turn on defined terms, and the definition decides the result.

2. **Change the design.** VM0047 lets one project hold instances under different approaches, if each instance meets one approach in full and a buffer of at least 10 meters separates them.

3. **Use a different methodology.** It must be listed on the Verra website, in a version that is active on the date the request is submitted.

4. **Ask for a methodology revision.** The Methodology Requirements treat a revision as appropriate where the activity is broadly similar to eligible ones. Under the [Methodology Development and Review Process, v5.1](https://verra.org/documents/methodology-development-and-review-process-all/), Verra leads the work and a stakeholder starts it with a methodology idea note. A scope-expanding major revision must show potential of at least 500,000 tCO2e a year within five years.

Gold Standard has one more route. Its [Deviation Request Requirements and Procedures, v2.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/110-par-deviation-approval-procedure/), accept requests to deviate from a standard requirement or a methodology before submission. The Secretariat decides where a precedent exists. Otherwise the Technical Advisory Committee decides, which the document expects to take four to eight weeks. Decisions are published and approval is not assured. Permanent changes to a certified project go through the [Design Change Request Requirements and Procedures, v2.0](https://globalgoals.goldstandard.org/111-par-design-change-requirements/).

After registration, a VCS project that moves to another methodology follows the [Procedure to Change Methodology through a Project Description Deviation, v4.0](https://verra.org/documents/vcs-methodology-change-project-deviation-procedure-v4-0-1/). It must meet every applicability condition of the new methodology.

## How do the conditions work on a real project?

The two illustrations below use invented projects and the published text of each methodology.

### VM0047 v1.1, census-based approach

A developer plans to plant trees on 8 hectares of smallholder cropland at 45 planting units per hectare. Under [VM0047, v1.1](https://verra.org/documents/vm0047-afforestation-reforestation-and-revegetation-v1-1/), the density limit allows up to 400 units on this instance, so 360 passes. Section 4.3 asks for less than 10% pre-existing woody biomass cover, and/or continuous cropping or a settlements or other lands category. Section 6.2 asks for the cover limit and one of those land uses together, so the developer should evidence both. Soil inversion for planting must stay within 25 cm and happen only once in the crediting period. Records and measurements settle these.

Two conditions need a person. Planting must be dispersed across the instance, not concentrated in part of it. And the pre-project land use must be maintained for the project lifetime, which is a claim about the future. At 80 units per hectare the instance fails the census-based approach and would have to meet the area-based conditions instead.

### VMR0017 v1.0

A 40 MW onshore wind farm is planned in a lower middle-income country that is not a least developed country. The default eligibility in the VCS Standard, v5.0, admits grid-connected wind only in least developed countries. [VMR0017, v1.0](https://verra.org/documents/vmr0017-grid-connected-electricity-generation-from-renewable-sources/), admits wind of any capacity in low-, lower middle- and upper middle-income countries, and states that its condition supersedes the default. The project passes. The same plant in a high-income country fails. A 20 MW hydroelectric plant fails everywhere, because hydro is limited to 15 MW and to least developed countries.

VMR0017 does not stand alone. It must be used with CDM methodology ACM0002, v22.0, and its tools carry their own conditions. Verra's [methodology page](https://verra.org/methodologies/vmr0017-grid-connected-electricity-generation-from-renewable-sources-acm0002-revision-v1-0/) states that projects requesting registration on or after 1 January 2027 must apply VMR0017 in full.

## How Climat handles this

Climat's applicability check evaluates every applicability condition of the chosen methodology as pass, fail or needs review. Conditions that call for judgment are routed to a person instead of being guessed. The methodologies available today are Verra VM0047 v1.1, census-based approach, and Verra VMR0017 v1.0.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a methodology deviation fix a failed applicability condition?

Not under the VCS Program. Both the VCS Standard, v4.7, and the VCS Standard, v5.0, permit methodology deviations only for monitoring and measurement and state that deviations from any other part of the methodology are not permitted. Gold Standard has a separate deviation request process, decided case by case.

### Do the applicability conditions of tools and modules count?

Yes. The VCS Methodology Requirements, v5.0, state that applicability conditions set out in tools or modules used by a methodology also apply. Verra's project description template asks for a separate table for each methodology, module and tool applied.

### When are applicability conditions assessed?

At validation. The VCS Methodology Requirements, v5.0, require conditions to be written so that eligibility can be determined at validation, without ongoing actions to stay eligible. A later change to the project that affects applicability is handled as a project description deviation and assessed at verification.

### Does a methodology's geographic condition override the VCS Standard's default eligibility?

Yes. Section 2.1.1 of the VCS Standard, v5.0, says a geographic applicability condition in a methodology takes precedence over the default eligibility in Table 1. VMR0017 v1.0 states that its technology and geography table supersedes that default.

### Can Verra grant an exemption from an applicability condition?

No. Verra's Exemption Request Procedure, v1.0, allows exemption requests only for deadlines, site visits by the validation and verification body, rotation and change of that body. It states that requests related to any other program rules will not be granted.

### What happens if a registered project stops meeting a condition?

Under the VCS Standard the change is a project description deviation. Where it affects the applicability of the methodology, the proponent describes and justifies it in a revised project description, and the validation and verification body assesses it at the next verification. Both the VCS Standard, v4.7, and the VCS Standard, v5.0, require a site visit at that verification.
