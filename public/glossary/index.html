Glossary of carbon project terms

Short definitions of the terms that appear in carbon project documents and in the standards that govern them. Each definition names the document it rests on. Where standards use a term differently, the definition says so.

68 terms · Updated September 30, 2026

ABCDEFGHILMNPRSUV

A

Activity method

Also: Positive list

An activity method is a standardized approach that determines additionality for a whole class of project activities instead of project by project. Under VCS Methodology Requirements v5.0 the methodology's applicability conditions act as a positive list: an activity that meets them and shows regulatory surplus is deemed additional. The methodology must justify the list through activity penetration of no more than 5%, a financial feasibility analysis or the absence of significant revenue other than credits.

Source: VCS Methodology Requirements v5.0, Verra. Related: Additionality, Applicability conditions, Regulatory surplus, Performance benchmark. Read the full explanation →

Additionality

Additionality is the requirement that a project's emission reductions or removals exceed what would happen under business as usual and would not have occurred without the incentive of carbon credit revenue. Under VCS Standard v5.0 it is demonstrated in the project description using the procedure in the applied methodology: a project method, a performance method or an activity method. Gold Standard also requires proof of financial additionality. The validation body assesses the demonstration.

Source: VCS Standard v5.0, Verra. Related: Regulatory surplus, Investment analysis, Common practice analysis, Baseline scenario. Read the full explanation →

Afforestation, reforestation and revegetation ARR

Afforestation, reforestation and revegetation (ARR) is the VCS project category for activities that increase carbon stocks in woody biomass, and in some cases soils, by establishing, increasing or restoring vegetative cover through planting, sowing or human-assisted natural regeneration of woody vegetation. VCS Program Definitions v5.0 place it in sectoral scope 14. ARR projects complete a non-permanence risk analysis and contribute buffer credits. Verra methodology VM0047 v1.1 covers ARR.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Agriculture, forestry and other land use, Non-permanence risk, Buffer pool, Removal versus reduction. Read the full explanation →

Agriculture, forestry and other land use AFOLU

Agriculture, forestry and other land use (AFOLU) is the group of VCS sectoral scopes covering emission reductions and carbon dioxide removals from land-based activities: scope 14, forestry and other land use, and scope 15, agriculture. VCS Standard v5.0 lists seven AFOLU project categories, including ARR, improved forest management, REDD and agricultural land management. AFOLU projects whose results come from carbon sinks must complete a non-permanence risk analysis and deposit buffer credits.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Afforestation, reforestation and revegetation, Non-permanence risk, Buffer pool, Leakage. Read the full explanation →

Applicability conditions

Applicability conditions are the criteria in a methodology that define which project activities may use it, such as geography, technology type or historical land use. Under VCS Methodology Requirements v5.0 they must be checkable at validation and may not impose ongoing obligations on the project proponent. Conditions in any tool or module the methodology uses also apply. The validation body assesses the project against each condition and records its conclusion in the validation report.

Source: VCS Methodology Requirements v5.0, Verra. Related: Methodology, Validation, Project description, Activity method. Read the full explanation →

Article 6.2

Also: Cooperative approaches

Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement is the provision under which countries cooperate voluntarily by transferring mitigation outcomes for use toward nationally determined contributions or other international mitigation purposes. Decision 2/CMA.3 sets the guidance: participating countries authorize the outcomes, track them as internationally transferred mitigation outcomes (ITMOs), apply corresponding adjustments and report to the UNFCCC. A project's credits can enter a cooperative approach only once the host country has authorized them.

Source: Decision 2/CMA.3: Guidance on cooperative approaches referred to in Article 6, paragraph 2, of the Paris Agreement, UNFCCC. Related: Internationally transferred mitigation outcome, Corresponding adjustment, Host country authorization, Article 6.4 mechanism.

Article 6.4 emission reduction A6.4ER

An Article 6.4 emission reduction (A6.4ER) is the unit issued under the Article 6.4 mechanism for one tonne of CO2 equivalent of mitigation. Under Decision 3/CMA.3 it may be used toward a nationally determined contribution or other international mitigation purposes only if the host country authorizes it and applies a corresponding adjustment. Units without authorization are mitigation contribution A6.4ERs, which Decision 6/CMA.6 lets the host country authorize later.

Source: Decision 3/CMA.3: Rules, modalities and procedures for the mechanism established by Article 6, paragraph 4, of the Paris Agreement, UNFCCC. Related: Article 6.4 mechanism, Corresponding adjustment, Host country authorization, Internationally transferred mitigation outcome.

Article 6.4 mechanism PACM

Also: Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism

The Article 6.4 mechanism, which the UNFCCC calls the Paris Agreement Crediting Mechanism (PACM), is the crediting mechanism established by Article 6, paragraph 4 of the Paris Agreement. Decision 3/CMA.3 sets its rules. A Supervisory Body of 12 members approves methodologies, registers activities and approves the issuance of Article 6.4 emission reductions. Each activity needs host country approval, and validation and verification by a designated operational entity.

Source: Decision 3/CMA.3: Rules, modalities and procedures for the mechanism established by Article 6, paragraph 4, of the Paris Agreement, UNFCCC. Related: Article 6.4 emission reduction, Article 6.2, Host country authorization, Crediting period.

B

Barrier analysis

Barrier analysis is an additionality test that shows a project activity faces barriers that would prevent it without carbon credit revenue, while at least one alternative does not face them. Verra's VT0008 v1.0 limits barriers to financial, information and institutional ones unless the methodology names others, and requires evidence that credit revenue is decisive in overcoming them. Some methodologies exclude the test: VMR0017 v1.0 does not allow barrier analysis.

Source: VT0008 Additionality Assessment v1.0, Verra. Related: Additionality, Investment analysis, Common practice analysis, Regulatory surplus. Read the full explanation →

Baseline emissions

Baseline emissions are the greenhouse gas emissions that would occur in the baseline scenario. They are the reference against which project emissions and leakage are compared to calculate emission reductions. The applied methodology prescribes the equations, data and default factors. VCS Standard v5.0 requires conservative choices, which for the baseline means lower emissions and higher removals. Estimates are given ex ante in the project document and calculated ex post at each verification.

Source: Glossary: CDM terms, version 11.0, UNFCCC. Related: Baseline scenario, Combined margin emission factor, Leakage, Ex-ante estimate.

Baseline reassessment

Baseline reassessment is the periodic check, within a crediting period, of whether a project's baseline scenario is still valid, followed by requantification of baseline emissions or carbon stocks. For registration requests from 1 January 2027, VCS Standard v5.0 sets a five-year interval for improved forest management and several conservation project types and leaves ARR to the applied methodology. The proponent applies current rules, shows regulatory surplus again and has the revised project description validated.

Source: VCS Standard v5.0, Verra. Related: Baseline scenario, Crediting period, Regulatory surplus, Methodology.

Baseline scenario

The baseline scenario is the description of the activities and greenhouse gas emissions that would occur in the absence of the project activity. Under VCS Standard v5.0 it is determined with the procedure in the applied methodology, must take account of government policies and legal requirements, and must not lead to overestimated reductions or removals. Gold Standard defines it as the reasonable, conservative scenario without the project. It is documented in the project description or PDD.

Source: VCS Standard v5.0, Verra. Related: Baseline emissions, Additionality, Baseline reassessment, Methodology.

Buffer pool

Also: AFOLU pooled buffer account, Compliance buffer

A buffer pool is a shared reserve of non-tradable credits that a registry holds to cover carbon stock losses across its projects. Under VCS Version 5, AFOLU projects deposit buffer credits in the AFOLU pooled buffer account at each issuance, in proportion to their risk rating, and Verra cancels buffer credits when a reversal occurs. Gold Standard requires land use and forest projects to place 20% of issued credits for sequestration in its buffer.

Source: VCS Standard v5.0, Verra. Related: Non-permanence risk, Reversal, Permanence, Agriculture, forestry and other land use.

C

Carbon credit

A carbon credit is a tradable instrument issued by a carbon-crediting program that represents one metric tonne of carbon dioxide equivalent reduced or removed, measured against a baseline scenario and adjusted for leakage. Each credit is serialized, issued, tracked and retired or canceled in an electronic registry. Verra's credit is the Verified Carbon Unit and Gold Standard's is the Verified Emission Reduction. This definition follows the ICVCM Core Carbon Principles v1.1.

Source: Core Carbon Principles, Assessment Framework and Assessment Procedure v1.1, Section 5: Definitions, ICVCM. Related: Verified Carbon Unit, Verified Emission Reduction, Vintage, Double counting.

Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation CORSIA

The Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation (CORSIA) is the global market-based measure of the International Civil Aviation Organization under which aircraft operators offset international emissions above their allowable share with eligible emissions units. ICAO decides which crediting programs, activity types and vintages are eligible for each three-year phase. Verra's CORSIA Label Guidance v1.1 requires Article 6 authorization for units with vintages from 2021 onward.

Source: CORSIA Label Guidance v1.1, Verra. Related: Host country authorization, Corresponding adjustment, Vintage, Verified Carbon Unit.

Clarification request CL

A clarification request (CL) is a finding raised by a validation/verification body when project reporting is unclear or incomplete and more information is needed to judge whether a requirement is met. Verra's Validation and Verification Manual v3.2 and the Gold Standard glossary use the term the same way. Every clarification request must be resolved before a positive validation or verification opinion is issued, and the report summarizes each one.

Source: Validation and Verification Manual v3.2, Verra. Related: Corrective action request, Forward action request, Validation, Verification. Read the full explanation →

Combined margin emission factor

Also: Grid emission factor

The combined margin emission factor is the weighted average of a grid's operating margin and build margin emission factors, in tonnes of CO2 per megawatt-hour, used to calculate the baseline emissions of projects that supply or save grid electricity. CDM TOOL07 v07.0 sets the calculation steps. Verra's VT0011 v1.0, which VMR0017 v1.0 requires, changes the weights: for wind and solar projects each margin counts 50% in the first crediting period.

Source: VT0011 Electricity System Emission Factors v1.0, Verra. Related: Baseline emissions, Crediting period, Methodology. Read the full explanation →

Common practice analysis

Common practice analysis is an additionality test that checks how far the proposed project type has already spread in the relevant sector and geographic area. Under Verra's VT0008 v1.0 a project is common practice, and so not additional, where similar projects without essential distinctions make up more than 20% of comparable projects and number more than three. CDM TOOL01 v07.0.0 uses the test as a credibility check on the investment or barrier analysis.

Source: VT0008 Additionality Assessment v1.0, Verra. Related: Additionality, Investment analysis, Barrier analysis, Regulatory surplus. Read the full explanation →

Core Carbon Principles CCP

Also: CCPs

The Core Carbon Principles (CCPs) are ten principles set by the Integrity Council for the Voluntary Carbon Market (ICVCM) to identify high-quality carbon credits. They cover governance, emissions impact and sustainable development, and include additionality, permanence, robust quantification and no double counting. The ICVCM assesses crediting programs and categories of credits against its Assessment Framework v1.1. Credits in approved categories, issued by eligible programs, may be tagged as CCP-Approved.

Source: Core Carbon Principles, Assessment Framework and Assessment Procedure v1.1, Section 2: Core Carbon Principles, ICVCM. Related: Additionality, Permanence, Double counting, Carbon credit.

Corrective action request CAR

A corrective action request (CAR) is a finding raised by a validation/verification body when it identifies a material discrepancy or a non-conformance that the project proponent must correct. Under Verra's Validation and Verification Manual v3.2 every CAR must be resolved before a positive opinion is issued, and the body must not advise on how to fix it. Gold Standard reviewers can also raise CARs during design review and performance review.

Source: Validation and Verification Manual v3.2, Verra. Related: Clarification request, Forward action request, Validation, Materiality. Read the full explanation →

Corresponding adjustment CA

A corresponding adjustment is the accounting entry a country makes to its emissions balance so that a transferred mitigation outcome is counted only once. Under Decision 2/CMA.3 a host country with an emissions target adds the outcomes it authorized and first transferred to its reported emissions, and the country using them subtracts them. Only authorized outcomes are adjusted. Verra accepts a host country's biennial transparency report as evidence that the adjustment was made.

Source: Decision 2/CMA.3: Guidance on cooperative approaches referred to in Article 6, paragraph 2, of the Paris Agreement, UNFCCC. Related: Internationally transferred mitigation outcome, Host country authorization, Article 6.2, Double counting.

Crediting period

The crediting period is the time span during which a project's verified emission reductions or removals are eligible for credit issuance. Table 8 of VCS Standard v5.0 sets 20 to 100 years for ARR, IFM, REDD and other land categories, renewable four times; five years renewable eight times for geological carbon storage; and five years renewable twice for energy and industry. Gold Standard certifies in five-year renewable cycles.

Source: VCS Standard v5.0, Verra. Related: Initial crediting period start date, Baseline reassessment, Vintage, Methodology.

D

Design certification

Also: Gold Standard Certified Project Design

Design certification is the Gold Standard milestone a project reaches after validation by an approved validation/verification body and a design review by Gold Standard that includes a two-week public consultation. Under Principles & Requirements v2.1 it confirms that the project design, safeguards, stakeholder consultation and monitoring plan meet GS4GG requirements. It does not certify any impact. Validation must be completed within two years of listing, and certification is renewed every five years.

Source: Gold Standard for the Global Goals Principles & Requirements v2.1, Gold Standard. Related: Gold Standard for the Global Goals, Validation, Project design document, Public comment period. Read the full explanation →

Double counting

Double counting is any situation in which the same emission reduction or removal is counted, claimed or credited more than once. VCS Program Definitions v5.0 divide it into double issuance, double use and double claiming, and the ICVCM and Gold Standard use the same three categories. VCS Standard v5.0 bars a project from registering while it is active in another program. Corresponding adjustments address double claiming by a host country.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Corresponding adjustment, Carbon credit, Core Carbon Principles, Verified Carbon Unit.

E

Ex-ante estimate

Also: Ex ante

An ex-ante estimate is a forecast of a project's emission reductions or removals made before they occur, based on assumptions instead of monitored results. It appears in the project description or PDD and is assessed at validation. It does not generate credits: registries issue credits only for results calculated ex post from monitored data and verified. VCS Standard v5.0 also uses the estimated annual volume to classify project scale and set some registration deadlines.

Source: Gold Standard for the Global Goals Glossary (25 July 2026), Gold Standard. Related: Ex-post, Validation, Project description, Baseline emissions.

Ex-post

Also: Ex post

Ex-post describes quantification done after the fact, from data monitored during a period that has already ended. VCS Program Definitions v5.0 define verification as an ex-post assessment of the reductions and removals that occurred in a monitoring period, based on historical data. Credits are issued only on verified ex-post results. Some parameters may be fixed ex ante at validation or updated ex post each year, as CDM TOOL30 v04.0 allows for fNRB.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Ex-ante estimate, Verification, Monitoring report, Fraction of non-renewable biomass.

F

Forward action request FAR

A forward action request (FAR) is a finding raised by a validation/verification body or registry reviewer on an issue that must be reviewed at a later audit. The Gold Standard glossary gives examples raised at validation, such as implementation status or the specifications of installed equipment, to be checked at the first verification. Verra's validation report template v5.0A records FARs alongside corrective action requests and clarification requests.

Source: Gold Standard for the Global Goals Glossary (25 July 2026), Gold Standard. Related: Corrective action request, Clarification request, Validation, Verification. Read the full explanation →

Fraction of non-renewable biomass fNRB

The fraction of non-renewable biomass (fNRB) is the share of the woody biomass consumed in an area that exceeds what the area can supply on a sustainable basis. Methodologies for projects that displace such biomass, for example improved cookstoves, use it to calculate baseline emissions. CDM TOOL30 v04.0 calculates it as non-renewable biomass divided by total woody biomass consumed and allows a default of 0.3. The value is fixed at validation or updated yearly.

Source: TOOL30: Calculation of the fraction of non-renewable biomass, version 04.0, UNFCCC. Related: Baseline emissions, Ex-ante estimate, Ex-post, Methodology.

Free, prior and informed consent FPIC

Free, prior and informed consent (FPIC) is a safeguard that protects the right of Indigenous Peoples, and of local communities holding customary rights, to grant or withhold consent to activities affecting their lands, territories and resources. Under VCS Standard v5.0 consent must be obtained before the project start date where such rights holders are present in the project area, and recorded in a signed project implementation agreement. These requirements apply to projects starting from 1 January 2027.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Safeguards, Stakeholder consultation, Project start date.

G

Gold Standard for the Global Goals GS4GG

Also: Gold Standard

Gold Standard for the Global Goals (GS4GG) is the certification standard of the Gold Standard Foundation for projects that deliver climate and sustainable development outcomes. Under Principles & Requirements v2.1 every project must contribute to at least three Sustainable Development Goals including SDG 13, meet safeguarding principles, consult stakeholders and pass design certification and performance certification. Projects run on five-year renewable certification cycles and may be issued Verified Emission Reductions.

Source: Gold Standard for the Global Goals Principles & Requirements v2.1, Gold Standard. Related: Design certification, Verified Emission Reduction, SDG contribution, Stakeholder consultation. Read the full explanation →

Grouped project

A grouped project is a VCS project with multiple project activity instances that is designed so that new instances can be added after registration. Under VCS Standard v5.0 the proponent sets eligibility areas and eligibility criteria at validation, establishes at least one initial instance in each area and adds later instances in batches at verification. New instances must share the baseline scenario and additionality conditions of the initial ones and meet every applicability condition.

Source: VCS Standard v5.0, Verra. Related: Project activity instance, Program of activities, Applicability conditions, Validation.

H

Host country authorization

Also: Letter of authorization, LoA, Host country authorisation

Host country authorization is the formal statement by which the government of the country hosting a project permits its mitigation outcomes to be used toward another country's nationally determined contribution or other international mitigation purposes. It is usually issued as a letter of authorization (LoA) and obliges the host country to apply a corresponding adjustment. Decision 4/CMA.6 lists its required content. Verra's Article 6 Label Guidance v1.1 requires signature by the designated national authority.

Source: Decision 4/CMA.6: Matters relating to cooperative approaches referred to in Article 6, paragraph 2, of the Paris Agreement, UNFCCC. Related: Corresponding adjustment, Internationally transferred mitigation outcome, Article 6.2, Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation.

I

Initial crediting period start date

Also: Crediting period start date

The initial crediting period start date is the first day of a VCS project's first crediting period, the date on which its reductions or removals begin to occur. VCS Program Definitions v5.0 use the term in place of crediting period start date. The first monitoring period begins on this date. For registration requests from 1 January 2027, deadlines run from it: five years for AFOLU projects and two years for energy and industry projects, with exceptions.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Crediting period, Project start date, Pipeline listing. Read the full explanation →

Internationally transferred mitigation outcome ITMO

An internationally transferred mitigation outcome (ITMO) is an emission reduction or removal that is real, verified and additional, achieved from 2021 onward, authorized by the host country and transferred internationally under Article 6.2 of the Paris Agreement. Decision 2/CMA.3 defines ITMOs and measures them in tonnes of CO2 equivalent or in another metric set by the participating countries. Authorized Article 6.4 emission reductions are also ITMOs. Each requires a corresponding adjustment.

Source: Decision 2/CMA.3: Guidance on cooperative approaches referred to in Article 6, paragraph 2, of the Paris Agreement, UNFCCC. Related: Article 6.2, Corresponding adjustment, Host country authorization, Article 6.4 emission reduction.

Investment analysis

Investment analysis is an additionality test that shows a project activity is not financially attractive, or not the most attractive alternative, without carbon credit revenue. Verra's VT0008 v1.0 allows an investment comparison analysis or a benchmark analysis based on internal rate of return, each with a sensitivity analysis. VCS Methodology Requirements v5.0 require input values that were valid at the time of the investment decision. The analysis is documented in the project description.

Source: VT0008 Additionality Assessment v1.0, Verra. Related: Additionality, Barrier analysis, Common practice analysis, Regulatory surplus. Read the full explanation →

L

Leakage

Leakage is the net change in greenhouse gas emissions that occurs outside the project boundary but is attributable to the project. VCS Methodology Requirements v5.0 name three types for land-based projects: activity-shifting, market and ecological leakage. Leakage is quantified with the applied methodology at each verification and deducted from the project's reductions and removals. Under VCS Standard v5.0 leakage outside the host country is not quantified and positive leakage cannot be claimed.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Project boundary, Baseline emissions, Agriculture, forestry and other land use, Methodology.

M

Materiality

Materiality is the concept used to decide whether errors, omissions and misstatements in a project's reported information could affect its GHG statement and the decisions based on it. Under VCS Standard v5.0 the threshold is 5% of total reported reductions and removals, or 1% for large projects, which the standard sets at more than 300,000 tonnes of CO2 equivalent per year. The validation/verification body tests the project's claims against this threshold.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Verification, Validation/verification body, Corrective action request. Read the full explanation →

Methodology

A methodology is the set of criteria and procedures, approved by a crediting program for specific project activities, for identifying the project boundary, determining the baseline scenario, demonstrating additionality, quantifying net reductions and removals, and specifying monitoring. Under VCS Standard v5.0 a project must apply an eligible methodology in full, in a version that is active on the date the registration request is submitted. Applicability conditions decide whether a project may use it.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Applicability conditions, Methodology deviation, Baseline scenario, Additionality.

Methodology deviation

A methodology deviation is a departure from the monitoring or measurement criteria and procedures of the applied methodology. VCS Standard v5.0 permits deviations only for monitoring and measurement, only at validation or verification, and only where they do not make the quantification less conservative unless they make it more accurate. The validation/verification body reports the deviation and its consequences in its report and in all later verification reports. Deviations do not set precedent.

Source: VCS Standard v5.0, Verra. Related: Methodology, Project description deviation, Monitoring plan, Verification.

Monitoring plan

Also: Monitoring and reporting plan

A monitoring plan is the part of the project document that sets out how the data needed to quantify reductions and removals will be obtained, recorded and checked during the crediting period. VCS Standard v5.0 requires it to include roles and responsibilities. Gold Standard requires, for each parameter, the metric, monitoring frequency, collection method and quality control. The plan is validated with the project document, and each monitoring report must follow it.

Source: VCS Standard v5.0, Verra. Related: Monitoring report, Methodology deviation, Verification, Project description. Read the full explanation →

Monitoring report

A monitoring report is the document in which the project proponent records the monitored data and the resulting emission reductions or removals for a specific monitoring period, following the monitoring plan. Under VCS Standard v5.0 it uses the VCS Monitoring Report Template, states the quantity generated in each calendar year and is verified before submission to Verra. Gold Standard monitoring reports also cover SDG impacts, safeguards and stakeholder feedback.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Monitoring plan, Verification, Vintage, Ex-post. Read the full explanation →

N

Non-permanence risk

Also: Reversal risk

Non-permanence risk is the risk that carbon stored in a pool such as biomass, soil or a geologic reservoir is released back to the atmosphere. Under VCS Version 5, AFOLU proponents assess internal, external and natural risks over 100 years with the AFOLU Non-Permanence Risk Tool at validation and each verification. The resulting rating, with a minimum of 12, sets the share of credits deposited in the buffer. A project with a rating above 60 fails.

Source: Procedure for Applying the AFOLU Non-Permanence Risk Tool v5.0, Verra. Related: Buffer pool, Permanence, Reversal, Agriculture, forestry and other land use.

P

Performance benchmark

Also: Performance method

A performance benchmark is a level of performance, expressed per unit of output or input or as a sequestration metric, against which individual projects are compared to determine additionality, the crediting baseline or both. It is the core of the VCS performance method defined in VCS Program Definitions v5.0. VM0047 v1.1 uses a dynamic performance benchmark, derived from observations of matched control plots, for ARR projects that apply its area-based approach.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Additionality, Activity method, Baseline scenario, Afforestation, reforestation and revegetation. Read the full explanation →

Permanence

Also: Durability

Permanence is the principle that credited emission reductions or removals must not be reversed, or that any reversal must be compensated. The ICVCM Core Carbon Principles v1.1 require measures that address reversal risk and compensate for reversals. VCS Version 5 applies the principle through risk analysis, buffer contributions and a rule that projects with non-permanence risk last, and are monitored, for at least 40 years. VCS Program Definitions v5.0 use the related term durability.

Source: Core Carbon Principles, Assessment Framework and Assessment Procedure v1.1, Section 2: Core Carbon Principles, ICVCM. Related: Non-permanence risk, Reversal, Buffer pool, Core Carbon Principles.

Pipeline listing

Also: Project pipeline, Listed status

Pipeline listing is the public listing of a project on a registry before it is registered. On the Verra Registry a project is listed as under development or as under validation, and the second status requires a complete project description and a contracted validation body. For listing requests from 1 January 2027, VCS Standard v5.0 requires submission within one year of the initial crediting period start date. Gold Standard grants Listed status after preliminary review.

Source: Registration and Issuance Process v5.0, Verra. Related: Public comment period, Initial crediting period start date, Validation, Project description. Read the full explanation →

Program of activities PoA

Also: Programme of activities, Programme of Activity

A program of activities (PoA) is a coordinated set of project activities registered under one framework, to which further activities can be added over time. Under the CDM a coordinating or managing entity runs the PoA and includes component project activities. Gold Standard's Programme of Activity Requirements v3.0 use the same structure with voluntary project activities (VPAs). VCS Version 5 documents do not use the term; the grouped project is Verra's comparable structure.

Source: Glossary: CDM terms, version 11.0, UNFCCC. Related: Grouped project, Gold Standard for the Global Goals, Project design document.

Project activity instance

Also: Instance

A project activity instance is the smallest standalone unit of a project activity that can generate emission reductions or removals in conformance with the applied methodology, such as one cookstove, one landfill gas facility or one discrete management unit of forest or farm. VCS Program Definitions v5.0 use the term to describe project design. A project may have one instance or many, and a grouped project can add new instances after registration.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Grouped project, Project boundary, Methodology.

Project boundary

The project boundary is the set of greenhouse gas sources, sinks and reservoirs that are counted for the project and baseline scenarios. Under VCS Standard v5.0 the applied methodology states which are included, optional or excluded, and the proponent must justify leaving out any relevant one. The CDM and Gold Standard definitions also cover the physical or geographic limits of the activity. VCS rules treat the project area as a separate concept.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Leakage, Methodology, Baseline scenario, Project activity instance.

Project description

Also: PD

The project description is the VCS document that describes a project's emission reduction or removal activities and shows how it conforms to the VCS Program rules and the applied methodology. It is prepared in the VCS Project Description Template, or a digital template in the Verra Project Hub, and is the document a validation/verification body validates. It sets out the baseline scenario, additionality, estimated reductions and removals, and monitoring. Other programs use a project design document.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Project design document, Validation, Monitoring plan, Project description deviation. Read the full explanation →

Project description deviation

A project description deviation is a departure from the project design, procedures or other specifications in the validated project description. VCS Standard v5.0 permits such deviations at verification. Where the deviation affects the applicability of the methodology, additionality or the baseline scenario, the proponent must revise the project description and have the change validated. Other deviations are described and justified in the monitoring report. No deviation may be used to claim more credits for a past period.

Source: VCS Standard v5.0, Verra. Related: Project description, Methodology deviation, Verification, Applicability conditions.

Project design document PDD

A project design document (PDD) is the document in which a project developer sets out the design of a carbon project in the form the crediting program prescribes. The CDM and Gold Standard use this name, and Verra calls its equivalent the project description. A Gold Standard PDD covers the baseline and project scenarios, SDG impacts, the safeguarding assessment, stakeholder consultation and a monitoring and reporting plan. It is the basis for validation.

Source: Glossary: CDM terms, version 11.0, UNFCCC. Related: Project description, Validation, Monitoring plan, Design certification. Read the full explanation →

Project proponent

Also: Project developer, Project participant

The project proponent is the individual or organization that has overall control of, and responsibility and accountability for, a VCS project, alone or together with other proponents. Under VCS Program Definitions v5.0 proponents must be able to demonstrate the right to operate the project and the right to its reductions and removals. They issue the registration and issuance representations. Gold Standard calls this role the project developer and the CDM calls it the project participant.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Project description, Validation/verification body, Verified Carbon Standard.

Project start date

The project start date is the date of the first significant action to implement the project activity. Unless the methodology sets other criteria, VCS Program Definitions v5.0 define that action as the first written commitment to implement or build the activity or, where there is none, the first physical action. The definition applies to registration requests from 1 January 2027. Gold Standard uses the earliest date on which the developer committed to project expenditure.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Initial crediting period start date, Stakeholder consultation, Crediting period. Read the full explanation →

Public comment period

Also: Public consultation

The public comment period is the window during which anyone may submit comments on a project's documents before validation is completed. On the Verra Registry it lasts 30 days and starts when the project is listed as under validation. The proponent must take due account of the comments, and the validation body evaluates the responses. Gold Standard runs a two-week public consultation during design review, in addition to the developer's own stakeholder consultation.

Source: Registration and Issuance Process v5.0, Verra. Related: Pipeline listing, Stakeholder consultation, Validation, Design certification. Read the full explanation →

R

Regulatory surplus

Regulatory surplus means the project activity is not required by law. VCS Program Definitions v5.0 set the test by country: in high-income countries the activity must not be mandated by any law or regulatory framework, and elsewhere it must not be mandated by one that is systematically enforced. It is demonstrated at validation, at crediting period renewal and at baseline reassessment, and is the first step of every VCS additionality method.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Additionality, Baseline reassessment, Activity method, Investment analysis. Read the full explanation →

Removal versus reduction

Also: Carbon dioxide removal, GHG emission reduction

A removal takes carbon dioxide out of the atmosphere and stores it durably, while a reduction lowers or avoids greenhouse gas emissions that would otherwise occur. VCS Program Definitions v5.0 define both as net of project and leakage emissions, in units of one metric tonne. Methodologies state which outcome an activity produces. VCS Standard v5.0 requires the two to be quantified and reported separately where the methodology provides for it.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Afforestation, reforestation and revegetation, Verified Carbon Unit, Buffer pool, Permanence.

Reversal

A reversal is a situation in which a project's net reductions and removals for a monitoring period are negative, meaning that carbon credited earlier has been lost. VCS Program Definitions v5.0 distinguish avoidable reversals, which the proponent could influence, from unavoidable ones such as natural disasters. Verra cancels buffer credits to cover a reversal. After an avoidable reversal, no further VCUs are issued to the project until the deficit is remedied.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Buffer pool, Non-permanence risk, Permanence, Monitoring report.

S

Safeguards

Also: Safeguarding principles

Safeguards are measures that prevent a project from causing environmental, social or governance harm and that uphold human rights. VCS Standard v5.0 requires a risk assessment before the project start date, mitigation measures, monitoring and an update at each verification. These rules apply to projects that start from 1 January 2027, and earlier projects follow v4.7. Gold Standard requires an assessment against nine safeguarding principles, documented in the PDD and monitored.

Source: VCS Standard v5.0, Verra. Related: Free, prior and informed consent, Stakeholder consultation, SDG contribution.

SDG contribution

Also: Sustainable development contribution, SDG impact

An SDG contribution is a demonstrated positive effect of a project on one or more of the United Nations Sustainable Development Goals. VCS Standard v5.0 requires projects to quantify contributions to targets and indicators of at least three SDGs for each monitoring period, or to verify concurrently under the CCB or SD VISta programs. Gold Standard requires positive impacts on at least three SDGs, one of which must be SDG 13.

Source: VCS Standard v5.0, Verra. Related: Gold Standard for the Global Goals, Safeguards, Monitoring report. Read the full explanation →

Stakeholder consultation

Also: Stakeholder engagement

Stakeholder consultation is the process of informing the people affected by or interested in a project and taking their input into account in its design. Gold Standard requires at least two rounds, a physical meeting and a feedback round of at least 30 days, and expects consultation to begin before the project start date. VCS Standard v5.0 calls the process stakeholder engagement and adds an engagement plan and a grievance redress mechanism.

Source: Stakeholder Consultation and Engagement Requirements v2.1, Gold Standard. Related: Public comment period, Safeguards, Free, prior and informed consent, Project start date. Read the full explanation →

U

Uncertainty deduction

Also: Conservativeness deduction, Uncertainty discount

An uncertainty deduction is a downward adjustment to quantified reductions or removals that keeps the credited amount conservative when measurement uncertainty is high. VCS Methodology Requirements v5.0 express uncertainty as the half-width of the 90% confidence interval. Where it exceeds 10% of the estimate the methodology must set a deduction, and where it exceeds 100% the result cannot be credited. The project applies the formula in its methodology, as in VM0047 v1.1.

Source: VCS Methodology Requirements v5.0, Verra. Related: Methodology, Monitoring report, Verification, Materiality. Read the full explanation →

V

Validation

Validation is the independent assessment of a project's design by a validation/verification body before registration. Under VCS Program Definitions v5.0 it determines whether the project conforms to the program rules and evaluates whether the assumptions, limitations and methods behind its forecast outcomes are reasonable. It covers the project description, includes a site visit and ends in a validation report with an opinion. Gold Standard follows validation with its own design review.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Verification, Validation/verification body, Project description, Corrective action request. Read the full explanation →

Validation/verification body VVB

Also: Auditor, Designated operational entity

A validation/verification body (VVB) is an independent organization approved by a crediting program to validate projects and verify their emission reductions and removals. Under VCS Program Guide v5.0 a VVB must be accredited under ISO 17029 or ISO 14065 by a member of the International Accreditation Forum, be approved by Verra for the relevant sectoral scope and sign an agreement with Verra. The CDM calls this role the designated operational entity.

Source: VCS Program Guide v5.0, Verra. Related: Validation, Verification, Materiality, Corrective action request. Read the full explanation →

Verification

Verification is the periodic, ex-post independent assessment by a validation/verification body of the emission reductions and removals that a project achieved during a monitoring period. Under VCS Standard v5.0 it gives reasonable assurance that the claim is materially correct, with a materiality threshold of 5%, or 1% for large projects. It ends in a verification report and opinion, which Verra reviews before VCUs are issued. Gold Standard follows verification with a performance review.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Validation, Monitoring report, Materiality, Validation/verification body. Read the full explanation →

Verified Carbon Standard VCS

Also: VCS Program

The Verified Carbon Standard (VCS) is the greenhouse gas crediting program operated by Verra. It sets the rules for developing projects, validating them, verifying their emission reductions and carbon dioxide removals, and issuing Verified Carbon Units on the Verra Registry. VCS Version 5 was released on 16 December 2025 and replaces Version 4. Each new requirement has its own effective date, often 1 January 2027. The program builds on ISO 14064-2, 14064-3 and 14065.

Source: VCS Program Guide v5.0, Verra. Related: Verified Carbon Unit, Methodology, Project proponent, Validation/verification body. Read the full explanation →

Verified Carbon Unit VCU

A Verified Carbon Unit (VCU) is the credit issued under the VCS Program. Each VCU is held in the Verra Registry and represents the account holder's right to claim one metric tonne of CO2 equivalent reduced or removed, as verified by a validation/verification body under the VCS Program rules. VCUs are retired when the owner uses them in a claim. They can carry labels, such as CORSIA or Article 6 Authorized labels.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Verified Carbon Standard, Carbon credit, Vintage, Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation. Read the full explanation →

Verified Emission Reduction VER

Also: GSVER, Gold Standard Verified Emission Reduction

A Verified Emission Reduction (VER) is the carbon credit issued by Gold Standard, formally a Gold Standard Verified Emission Reduction (GSVER), for emission reductions or removals that have been verified and certified. GSVERs are denominated in tonnes of CO2 equivalent and issued after performance certification under GS4GG and its GHG Emissions Reduction & Sequestration Product Requirements v3.2. They are issued, held, transferred and retired on the Gold Standard Impact Registry.

Source: GHG Emissions Reduction & Sequestration Product Requirements v3.2, Gold Standard. Related: Gold Standard for the Global Goals, Carbon credit, Verified Carbon Unit, Design certification. Read the full explanation →

Vintage

Also: Vintage period

Vintage is the period in which the emission reductions or removals behind a credit occurred. VCS Program Definitions v5.0 define a vintage period as a calendar year, or part of one where a verification period starts or ends mid-year. It is not the year of issuance, because credits are issued after verification. Vintage affects eligibility: CORSIA phases accept only certain vintages, and Verra's Article 6 labels require reductions or removals from 2021 onward.

Source: VCS Program Definitions v5.0, Verra. Related: Verified Carbon Unit, Monitoring report, Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation, Carbon credit.
