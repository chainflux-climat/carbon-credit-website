What is a project design document?

A project design document (PDD) is the document in which a developer describes a carbon project and demonstrates that it meets the rules of a carbon standard and of the methodology it applies. It is written before validation, audited by a validation/verification body (VVB), and published by the registry.

The Clean Development Mechanism (CDM) defines the PDD in its Glossary: CDM terms, version 11.0 as the document prepared by the project participant that sets out in detail the project activity to be undertaken. Verra's VCS Program Definitions v5.0 define the project description as the document that describes the project's GHG emission reduction or carbon dioxide removal activities and that uses Verra's template.

The document has three uses. It is the evidence a VVB assesses at validation. It fixes the baseline scenario, the additionality case, the estimated reductions or removals, and the monitoring plan. It is the reference for every later monitoring report and verification.

What is the document called under each standard?

Verra calls it a project description. Gold Standard, the CDM and the Article 6.4 mechanism call it a project design document. The function is the same. The template and the section order are not.

Which VCS template applies, v5.0A or v5.0B?

The project start date decides. Verra released VCS Version 5 on 16 December 2025 and published two versions of each template on 9 June 2026.

TemplateApplies toRules reported against
v5.0AProjects with a start date before 1 January 2027 that submit a pipeline listing request, a registration request, or a crediting period renewal or baseline reassessment before 1 January 2030VCS Version 5, except that ownership, stakeholder engagement and safeguards follow sections 3.7, 3.18 and 3.19 of the VCS Standard v4.7
v5.0BProjects with a start date on or after 1 January 2027, and earlier projects at a crediting period renewal or baseline reassessment submitted on or after 1 January 2030VCS Version 5 in full, including sections 3.6, 3.17 and 3.18 of the VCS Standard v5.0

Verra's release notice of 9 June 2026 states that the v5.0 templates must be used for all project requests submitted after 1 January 2027, including projects whose audits are already underway. The VCS Version 5 overview of updates and effective dates adds that a proponent who chooses to be validated to Version 5 before that date must use them too.

The previous template, v4.4 of April 2024, now sits on Verra's Version 4 archive page, while Verra lists the VCS Standard v4.7 as active until 1 January 2027. For a request submitted before that date under v4.7, confirm with Verra or the VVB which template to use.

Section 3.4.1 of the VCS Standard v5.0 also provides for a digital project description template in the Verra Project Hub where the methodology has been digitalized. The June 2026 notice says digital versions of the Version 5 templates are still being built and that Verra will announce the date from which they become mandatory.

What sections does a VCS project description contain?

Templates v5.0A and v5.0B each have five numbered sections and two standing appendices. The instructions inside the template are binding: section 3.4.1 of the VCS Standard v5.0 requires proponents to conform to all instructional text.

SectionWhat it has to establish
1. Project DetailsWhat the project is, who the proponent is, where the project is, and that it is eligible: sectoral scope, start date, crediting period, scale, design, conditions before the project, legal compliance, and no double counting or double claiming. v5.0A covers ownership here. v5.0B covers the right to operate and the right to reductions and removals.
2. Safeguards and Stakeholder EngagementHow stakeholders were consulted, whether free, prior and informed consent applies, how grievances are handled, and how risks to people and ecosystems are managed. v5.0B renames the section and draws on two standalone documents, the stakeholder engagement plan and the ESG risk assessment.
3. Application of MethodologyThat the methodology is active and applicable, with each applicability condition justified separately. Then the project boundary, the baseline scenario, additionality including regulatory surplus, and any methodology deviations.
4. Quantification of Estimated Reductions and RemovalsBaseline, project and leakage emissions, the non-permanence risk analysis and buffer allocation where relevant, and the estimated net reductions and removals.
5. MonitoringThe data and parameters, and the monitoring plan: data collection and management, calibration, personnel, internal auditing and QA/QC, handling of non-conformances, and sampling.
AppendicesSensitive information to be withheld from the public version, and the tables of data and parameters fixed at validation or monitored.

What sections does a Gold Standard PDD contain?

The Gold Standard PDD template v2.0 opens with key project information and a project impact summary, followed by lettered sections. The Principles & Requirements v2.1 require the PDD to carry the safeguarding assessment, the record of stakeholder consultation and the Monitoring & Reporting Plan.

SectionWhat it has to establish
A. Project descriptionLocation, technology or measures, funding structure, product ownership, eligibility, double counting risks, alignment with national climate and development policies, start date and crediting period.
B. Application of methodology(ies)Applicability, project boundary, baseline scenario, additionality (prior consideration, regulatory surplus, financial additionality, common practice), risk of reversals, calculation of emission reductions or net removals, and the monitoring plan.
C. Safeguarding principles and gender sensitivity assessmentThe risks identified against Gold Standard's safeguarding principles, with mitigation and monitoring.
D. Gender equality assessmentHow the project meets the gender requirements, and the related monitoring.
E. Sustainable development contributionThe contribution to at least three Sustainable Development Goals, one of which must be SDG 13, and how each is monitored.
F. Stakeholder consultation assessmentWho was invited, how the consultation ran, the comments received, how they were considered, and the continuous input and grievance mechanism.
G to IContact information, appendices, and additional information for land use and forests projects.

Who writes the PDD and who reads it?

The developer writes it and answers for it. A VVB audits it. The registry reviews the audit and publishes the document.

  • Author. Verra calls this party the project proponent and Gold Standard calls it the project developer. The VCS Program Guide v5.0 (section 2.6.1) makes the proponent responsible for providing the project description and supporting documentation.
  • Validation/verification body. The VVB assesses the document against the standard and the methodology and issues a validation report. See validation and verification.
  • Registry. Verra reviews each request to determine whether the VVB has appropriately assessed the project (Registration and Issuance Process v5.0, section 4.3). Gold Standard runs a design review in which the documents are open to its Technical Advisory Committee, its NGO supporters and the public.
  • The public. Under section 3.4.7 of the VCS Standard v5.0, all information in project documents is presumed public. Gold Standard publishes project documentation on its Impact Registry, except confidential information.

How does a PDD differ from a monitoring report?

The PDD is a forecast, written for validation. A monitoring report is a record, written for each monitoring period and audited at verification. Section 3.4 of the VCS Standard v5.0 says proponents prepare a project description for validation and a monitoring report for verification.

Under the VCS Standard v5.0 the monitoring report must state the reductions and removals generated in each calendar year of the monitoring period (section 3.4.3), and monitoring periods must not overlap (section 3.4.5).

What causes validation findings on a PDD?

Neither Verra nor Gold Standard publishes a ranking of the most frequent findings. The standards do state what a VVB must raise, and the templates state what a compliant answer looks like. The causes below come from those texts.

  • Applicability conditions answered as a group. The VCS template requires each condition to be justified separately, for every methodology, module and tool applied.
  • An inactive methodology version. Under section 3.1.4 of the VCS Standard v5.0, the version applied must be active on the date the registration request is submitted.
  • An additionality analysis that cannot be reproduced. The VCS template asks for enough data and parameters for a reader to reproduce the analysis and obtain the same results.
  • Sections deleted or marked "not applicable". The VCS template requires an explanation and a justification instead.
  • Core information marked confidential. Section 3.4.9 of the VCS Standard v5.0 bars proponents from withholding the baseline scenario, additionality, quantification, monitoring, or the non-permanence risk score.
  • Errors in the baseline, the parameters or the additionality assumptions. Gold Standard's Validation and Verification Standard v2.0 (paragraph 6.3.14) names these as grounds for a corrective action request, together with non-compliance in stakeholder consultation, safeguarding or the sustainable development assessment.
  • Weak technical detail. Verra's Validation and Verification Manual v3.2 (section 3.4) lists four common sources of material error: incomplete identification of GHG sources, sinks and reservoirs; calibration; emission factors, units and conversion factors; and models.

Whether a given gap becomes a finding is the VVB's judgment. The manual dates from 2016 and is guidance, not a rule, but section 4.1.8 of the VCS Standard v5.0 expects VVBs to follow it.

How Climat handles this

Climat drafts each section of the registry's project document in the registry's own order: the VCS project description or the Gold Standard for the Global Goals PDD. Statements carry citations to the intake data, to excerpts of the uploaded evidence, or to the methodology, and every section is reviewed, edited and finalized by a person. Climat is not a registry and not a validation body, and it does not replace validation or verification.
