Where do the facts come from?

From primary documents: the standard, the methodology, the template or the fee schedule, as published by the body that issues it. Each page lists the documents it rests on, with the version and the date we read them. Consultancy articles and news reports are not used as the source for a rule or a number.

If we cannot find a claim in a primary document, we leave it out. A shorter page that is right is worth more to a developer than a longer one that is nearly right.

How is AI used in writing these pages?

Pages are drafted with AI assistance. The draft is then checked, claim by claim, against the text of the documents it cites. We say this on every page, because a reader deciding how far to rely on a page is entitled to know how it was made.

AI assistance does not change the standard a page is held to. A claim is either in the source or it is not.

Who reviews a page?

Where a named practitioner has reviewed a page, the page gives their name and role beside the date. Where no practitioner has reviewed it yet, the page says so at the foot. We do not attribute a page to a person who did not do the work.

What do the dates mean?

The date on a page is the date of its last substantive change: a corrected rule, a new version of a standard, a rewritten section. It is not updated for a change of wording or layout. Each source carries the date we last opened it.

What happens when a standard changes?

Pages name the version they describe, so an out-of-date page is visibly out of date instead of silently wrong. When a registry publishes a new version, the page is revised against it and re-dated.

How do product pages differ?

Pages about Climat describe only what the product does today, checked against the application itself. Features that are in development are labeled as in development. Educational pages mention the product once, at the end.

How do I report an error?

Write to contact@chainflux.com with the page and the clause of the document that contradicts it. We will correct the page and date the change.
