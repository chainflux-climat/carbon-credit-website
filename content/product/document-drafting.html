What does Climat draft?

It drafts the project document the registry requires: the project description under the Verra Verified Carbon Standard, or the project design document under the Gold Standard for the Global Goals. Sections are written in the registry's own order, and the two registries do not ask for the same sections. See the VCS project description and the Gold Standard PDD.

Where does each statement come from?

Every statement in a draft points to one of three places.

Citation points toExampleHow it is checked
The intakeProject area, planting density, installed capacityThe value is the one you entered
An excerpt of your evidenceA page of a land record, a row of a censusThe excerpt named must be one that was supplied, or the citation is flagged
The methodologyA condition, an equation, a required parameterDrawn from the methodology as set up from its published text

What happens when the intake and the evidence disagree?

The disagreement is raised as a warning on the section it affects. If the intake says two species were planted and the uploaded census shows three, the draft does not pick one. It tells you, with the figures, so that you can correct whichever is wrong.

What happens when evidence is missing?

The section says so. An additionality test with no supporting document is marked as needing evidence. The draft does not fill the gap with a plausible figure. The same gaps appear earlier, in the evidence gap report, so that most of them can be closed before drafting starts.

Who has the last word?

You do. Each section is read, edited and finalized by a person. A finalized section is never regenerated. For background on what each section has to establish, see what a project design document is.
