Where is a language model used?

Part of the productHow it works
Intake formBuilt from the methodology's required inputs. Not generated.
Applicability checkRule-based. Computed from the methodology's conditions and your inputs.
Evidence gap reportRule-based. Computed from what the case needs and what was supplied.
Section draftingLanguage model, working only from your intake, excerpts of your evidence, and the methodology.
Conflict warningsLanguage model, comparing the intake with the uploaded evidence.
Citation checkRule-based. A cited excerpt must be one that was supplied.
Cover noteLanguage model, with the disclosure of AI assistance required.

How are citations checked?

When a draft cites an uploaded document, it names an excerpt. Climat checks that the excerpt named is one that was actually given to the draft. If it is not, the citation is marked unverified and the section is flagged for review. This catches the most damaging failure of generated text: a reference to a source that was never there.

It does not prove that the source supports the statement. That is what the human review is for.

What does a person have to do?

Read every section, correct it, and finalize it. Decide every applicability condition that is marked as needing review. Resolve every conflict warning. The document is the project proponent's, and so is the responsibility for what it says.

Whose model is it, and where is my data processed?

Drafting uses a large language model run by a third-party inference provider. Ask us which provider and model are in use and where they are hosted. We will tell you before you upload anything.

Is my organization's data kept separate?

Yes. Projects, documents and evidence are scoped to the organization that owns them in the data layer, so that one organization's requests cannot read another's records.

What can go wrong?

A language model can misread a document, draw a wrong conclusion from a correct source, or state something with more confidence than the evidence allows. The checks above reduce that risk and do not remove it. Treat a draft as a draft.
